> [!info]+ Quick facts
> 🌸 The 7 Colored Flowers are Asgore and Toriel's wedding bouquet, kept artificially alive for years at Flower King — Flowery is the golden flower; the other six are Aqua (cyan), Seth (purple), Orange (orange), Green (green), Yellow (yellow), and Blue (blue).
> 💍 Asgore kept them alive well beyond their natural lifespan of "one smiling day" out of pure affection, without knowing what his flower shop's Dark Fountain would eventually do with them.
> 👀 They've spent years secretly watching Asriel and Kris grow up — Kris even told them about the outside world and made deals with one of them (see Seth, below) long before Ch.5.
> 🎭 In the Dark World of Ch.5 (Flower Kingdom) they take on their own humanoid form; in the Light World, the six still look like simple flowers identical to one another save for color.
> 🃏 The seven represent the seven human SOULs from Undertale — the six "sisters" directly mirror Patience, Perseverance, Bravery, Kindness, Justice, and Integrity, each with the color, attack pattern, and even the equippable item proper to that SOUL. Flowery, the seventh and their leader, represents Determination — the same virtue that has no SOUL of its own tied to Flowey in Undertale.
> 💐 Pink is not one of the original 7 despite acting like one more member of the group — she's a separate case (see [[Mad Mew Mew (Pink)]]).
> 🎬 In the final battle against Flowery, the six reappear to empower him, in a clear mirror of the opening sequence of the Omega Flowey fight in Undertale, where the seven SOULs surrounded Flowey.

> [!tip]- Personality profile
> Each one tries, in her own way, to understand and "pass" as human by copying only the most superficial part of what she sees — the result is almost always a clumsy, well-meaning imitation. They all share the same underlying goal: to please Asgore and, in their own way, return the affection of having been kept alive.

## Where they live in the Light World
In the Light World, the 7 Flowers are still just potted flowers, kept on the second floor of Flower King — Asgore's own flower shop. That's where they remain, without any sentience of their own, until the Dark Fountain of Ch.5 gives them life in their Dark World version.

## The six flowers, one by one

> [!example]+ Aqua — Patience
> The cyan flower of the bouquet, with the SOUL of Patience. She's the only flower, aside from Flowery, with more than one color in her design (red and cyan) — up to three red eyes hidden beneath the one visible one. Seth was the one who gave her her name. She appears almost always alongside Seth in the Garden and the Cliffs, clumsily and literally trying to understand and imitate human behavior — she's the most childish and impulsive of the six, and despite representing Patience, she's the least patient of them all: she treats everything like a game, including her own fight against the group. She attacks by throwing knives, and at one point ruins a joint plan with Seth by making the wind blow upward instead of sideways. Her strongest attack is called "Omega Knife" — a direct nod to Omega Flowey from Undertale. After the final battle, she lends her power to Flowery's ultimate attack.

> [!example]+ Seth — Perseverance
> The purple flower, with the SOUL of Perseverance, who tends to pair up with Aqua for much of the chapter. He wears glasses, is very intelligent, and his greatest wish is to "become a genius." He attacks with books that fire projectiles and with his own glasses, which double the damage of any attack that passes through them. He can remove, counter, or discard the player's Mercy in battle — one of the few flowers able to directly interfere with that mechanic. He chose his own name instead of simply going by "Purple," something no other flower did — and he was also the one who gave Aqua her name, in a small gesture of sisterhood between the two.
>
> His great weakness is idolizing [[Berdly]] as the "avian genius" he aspires to resemble — he even imitates his poses without noticing, and nearly drools literally when Kris offers him one of Berdly's feathers as payment for his help. In fact, long before Ch.5, Kris had already told his Light World version about Berdly and negotiated her/his future cooperation in exchange for that same feather — one of the clearest pieces of evidence that Kris's relationship with the Flowers goes back much further than when they took on human form in Ch.5.

> [!example]+ Orange — Bravery
> The only one of the six without a humanoid form: she's a small orange-furred rat/mouse, with a bandana and boxing gloves. Her SOUL is Bravery, though ironically it costs her a great deal to actually be brave — her own confessed dream, when saying goodbye to the group, is to someday find real courage. She's described as Flowery's "little sister," though it isn't clear whether it's an actual blood relation or just a chosen family. She also has her own subplot with [[Mad Mew Mew (Pink)|Pink]], a pink flower who isn't officially one of the 7 but whom Orange calls her "big sister" — she can be found visiting and chatting with her at her tea stand.

> [!example]+ Green — Kindness
> The green flower, with the SOUL of Kindness. Of ambiguous gender and few words, she/he runs several of the restaurants and food stands scattered across the Flower Kingdom. She attacks by throwing fried eggs and fire from her frying pan — a direct nod to the "burnt pan and stained apron" pattern of the green SOUL in Undertale. She's one of the few who insistently (though without malice) remembers that the rest of the group owes her money — she'll even hold up a sign literally accusing whoever hasn't paid of "crime," her particular way of being kind without ceasing to be practical. Despite her role as an almost always serious provider, she's seen giving Ralsei a spontaneous hug and fist-bumping Susie — among the few gestures of direct warmth she hands out on her own.

> [!example]+ Yellow — Justice
> The yellow flower, with the SOUL of Justice. She fulfills her dream of being a cowgirl by dealing out "justice" her own way, as peculiar as it is naive — she's extremely gullible, to the point of believing any bald-faced lie Susie tells her and "sentencing" herself for it, locking herself alone in her own cell for "not living up to" justice. She attacks with a revolver, and her battle includes its own trial mechanic (the "Justice" ACT), with several distinct "cases" where you must choose between "the kind, good path" or "the path that punishes you" — a staging almost like a courtroom, fitting with her theme. She can be found searching for "bluegrass" as a gift for Blue, not realizing Blue meant the musical genre and not literally blue-colored grass. In the west wing of Flower Castle she gives the group a key to keep moving forward, right before the misunderstanding that triggers her self-punishment.

## Theories

> [!question]+ Why does Flowery get Determination, and not another virtue like her sisters?
> The other six directly mirror the color, attack pattern, and equippable item of six of the seven human SOULs from Undertale. Flowery, the seventh and the one who acts as leader, represents the remaining one: Determination — the same virtue that, in Undertale, defines Flowey precisely because he does *not* have a SOUL of his own ("no mercy, no compassion... yet an unbelievable amount of determination"). [TODO: verify exact EN quote] The most widespread reading is that this choice deliberately reinforces her corrupted nature compared to her "sisters": while they remain faithful to virtues with a positive connotation, Flowery ends up with the only one of the seven that, with no SOUL attached, can turn dangerous.
>
> ![[flowery_BromideR_item.png]]
> *The BromideR collectible item: Flowery holds a bouquet with her six "sisters" and, apart from it, a flower of her own — Determination set apart from the rest of the virtues.*

> [!question]- Is Noelle, deep down, one more "key" — like the one Yellow hands out?
> In Ch.5, Yellow gives the group an actual Yellow Key in the west wing of Flower Castle so they can move forward — her SOUL is Justice. Quite separately, on the secret Sword Route of Ch.3 (via the Ice Key), the White Cloak — which literally represents Noelle within that minigame — accompanies the player and is consumed at the exact same door as the key itself: the game shows the two messages back to back, *"The ice key was used up. She was used up."* [TODO: verify exact EN quote] In other words, Noelle is treated, textually, as a second key that gets "used up" just like an item.
>
> ![[noelle_she_was_used_up.png]]
> *The literal message that accompanies the White Cloak as it's consumed at the door — treating her as one more key.*
>
> ![[noelle_key_theory_yellow_key.webp]]
> *The same kind of message — "(The Yellow Key got used up.)" [TODO: verify exact EN quote] — appears with Yellow's own Yellow Key, the direct basis for the parallel.*
>
> The coincidence fueling the theory is the wordplay "JUST-ICE" (Justice = JUST + ICE) that Undertale already used for the SOUL of Integrity — combining exactly the two elements of this parallel: Yellow's Justice and the Ice associated with Noelle (the White Cloak, the IceShock, the Rare Route Snowgrave itself). Under that reading, the pattern of "something/someone being used as a key and used up" would repeat on purpose between Ch.3 (Noelle-as-key) and Ch.5 (Yellow's Yellow Key), tied together by the same phonetic joke Undertale had already planted years earlier. **A very speculative fan theory, supported by a textual pattern and a curious wordplay — there is no direct confirmation from Toby Fox that the two things are deliberately connected.**

> [!question]- Who does each Flower mirror, beyond the SOUL she represents?
> Beyond the parallel with Undertale's SOULs, a good part of the fandom also reads a second layer of mirroring: each Flower would also reflect a specific character from Deltarune's main cast. The strongest piece of evidence is Asgore's own scene near the end of Ch.5, where the colors associated with several members of the Dreemurr family are "checked" on screen — that's where it's confirmed that **Toriel is blue**, meaning that **Blue mirrors her**.
>
> From there, the rest are fan readings with varying levels of textual support:
> - **Green ↔ Asriel**: Green mentions the $5 that can be stolen from Asriel's drawer in Ch.2, and no one in town has anything bad to say about Green — the same treatment Asriel gets.
> - **Aqua ↔ Kris**: the most cited parallel of all — in her one-on-one battle, Aqua literally mimics Kris's movements, even more so if the ribbon is equipped.
> - **Seth ↔ Ralsei**: they both wear glasses and carry a book like the purple SOUL, but several fan analyses point out that Seth's own design and attitude (perseverant, intellectual, always paired with Aqua) recall Ralsei more than any other character — who is himself, in turn, already a mirror of Asriel.
> - **Kris, split across several Flowers at once**: there's no single clear candidate — Orange also mirrors him (her obsession with "being human" despite her rat-like appearance, like Kris with the horn headbands, and her fear of mice connects with Noelle's fear of the same), Blue mirrors his elegance while dancing, and Flowery himself mirrors his charisma for flirting with/sparing enemies.
> - **Rudy**: several fan screenshots point out poses, expressions, and hairstyles (mirrored) similar between Rudy and one of the Flowers — possibly Flowery, who also uses the same nicknames Rudy gives Asgore — though this is the least developed connection of all.
>
> **None of this is confirmed by Toby Fox beyond the Toriel/Blue fact itself — the rest are fan readings with varying degrees of visual and textual support, and in some cases (like Seth or Kris) several different readings compete with each other without any being definitive.**

## Trivia
- Several items and objects in the game mention the Flowers even before formally introducing them — the GreenTea, the OrangeJuice, or the biography of a recruitable Darkner who confesses to idolizing Green.
- For each one's Defense stat, her equippable item is also her own character accessory: Aqua's ribbon, Seth's glasses, Orange's bandana, Green's apron, Yellow's hat, and Blue's tutu.
- Although the flowers aren't technically male or female (they're plants), each one chooses her own gender based on her own idea of what she understands to be human — Seth is the only one who never fully settles on either.
