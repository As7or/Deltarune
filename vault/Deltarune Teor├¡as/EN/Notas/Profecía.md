> [!info]+ Quick facts
> 📜 Prophetic text present literally within the game, found as crystal stained-glass panes in the Dark Sanctuaries of Ch.4.
> 👑 Announces the gathering of three heroes — a human, a monster, and a prince of darkness — to seal the fountains and "banish the Angel's Heaven."
> ⚔️ Includes the duel against the Knight, referred to only by title, never by name.
> 🎤 Also revealed in Ch.4 as a hymn sung in the Light World, and as a "reading" given by Catty in church.
> ✒️ The only character mentioned by name in the entire legend is Jockington.

> [!tip]- The full text
> > Long ago, a LEGEND was whispered among the shadows.
> > This is the legend of DELTA RUNE.
> > For millennia, LIGHT and DARK have lived in balance,
> > bringing peace to the WORLD.
> > But should this harmony be broken...
> > a terrible calamity would occur.
> > The sky will be black with terror
> > and the earth will crack with fear.
> > Then, with its heart pounding hard...
> > the EARTH will draw its last breath.
> > Only then, shining with hope...
> > Three HEROES will appear at the World's edge.
> > A HUMAN, a MONSTER,
> > and a PRINCE OF DARKNESS.
> > Only they can seal the fountains
> > and banish the ANGEL'S HEAVEN.

## The Weird Route reinterprets it

This is the first time the game hints that this "script" can bend: the hidden choices that accumulate chapter by chapter (see [[Rutas|Routes]]) suggest the Prophecy can be fulfilled in ways very different from the lethal literalness of its text.

## Analysis of the Ch.4 stained-glass panes

The Dark Sanctuaries of Ch.4 scatter **35 crystal panes/stained-glass windows** across the area, each with its own image and line of text — together they form a much longer, rhymed-verse version of the Prophecy than the one Ralsei summarizes in Ch.1. The 35 gifs for each pane are stored in `Sprites/` (numbered 01 through 35, in the exact order they appear). This section analyzes them one by one, grouped by thematic block.

> [!info]- How to read this section
> Each entry gives the original phrase in English (the language the asset is in) and what it connects to elsewhere in the vault. When the connection is a reasoned reading of mine based on the text itself, I mark it as such — the text itself is 100% official; the interpretation of what each individual line means is not always.

### Block 1 — The world's origin (panes 1-6)

This first batch is, in essence, a verse reformulation of the "Legend of Delta Rune" that Ralsei already summarizes in Ch.1 — but in more detail, including the first in-game text that directly connects the Roaring to the Titans.

> [!example]- 01 — "The Prophecy, which whispers among the shadows."
> ![[01_la_profecia_que_susurra.gif]]
> Opening title of the whole sequence.

> [!example]- 02 — "The Legend of this World. &lt;DELTARUNE.&gt;"
> ![[02_la_leyenda_de_este_mundo.gif]]
> Presents the game's own name within the fiction, just as the Ch.1 prose version already does.

> [!example]- 03 — "A world basked in purest light. Beneath it, grew eternal night."
> ![[03_mundo_banado_en_luz_pura.gif]]
> The verse equivalent of "for millennia, Light and Dark have lived in balance" from the text Ralsei recites.

> [!example]- 04 — "If Fountains freed, the Roaring cries. And Titans shape from darkened eyes."
> ![[04_si_las_fuentes_se_liberan_el_rugido.gif]]
> **The first direct textual mention of the Roaring and the Titans within the Prophecy itself** — until now only Ralsei had explained it out loud in Ch.2 (see [[Titan]]). Confirms that the Ch.4 Titan isn't a one-off script whim, but the literal fulfillment of this prophetic line.

> [!example]- 05 — "The Light and Dark, both burning dire. A countdown to the Earth's expire."
> ![[05_luz_y_oscuridad_ambas_ardiendo.gif]]
> Corresponds to "the earth will draw its last breath" from the prose text.

> [!example]- 06 — "But lo, on hopes and dreams they send. Three Heroes at the World's End."
> ![[06_con_esperanzas_y_suenos_tres_heroes.gif]]
> The formal announcement of the three heroes — from here begins the block that introduces them one by one.

### Block 2 — The three heroes, numbered and described (panes 7-13)

This is, by far, the most important block for the vault's theories: the Prophecy explicitly numbers the three heroes as First, Second, and Third, and describes each one with a specific image — something the Ch.1 prose version never does.

> [!example]- 07 — "The Third Hero. The Prince, alone in deepest dark"
> ![[07_el_tercer_heroe_el_principe.gif]]
> The "prince of darkness" from the prose text, identified here as the **third** hero — almost certainly [[Ralsei]].

> [!example]- 08 — "The Prince, alone in deepest dark..."
> ![[08_the_prince_alone_in_deepest_dark.gif]]
> Continuation/variant of the previous pane, with the image now lacking the "Third Hero" header.

> [!example]- 09 — "The First Hero. The Cage, with human SOUL and parts!"
> ![[09_the_first_hero_the_cage_with_human_soul_and_parts.gif]]
> Describes the **first** hero — the "human" from the prose text — not as a person, but as **"the Cage"**: a body containing a human SOUL. It's one of the most-cited lines in the whole game for theories about [[Kris]] and their relationship to the Player — the image of a body as a container/cage for a SOUL that isn't its own fits almost too literally with Kris's own mechanic of tearing the SOUL from their chest at the end of several chapters.

> [!example]- 10 — "The Cage, with human SOUL and parts!"
> ![[10_the_cage_with_human_soul_and_parts_2.gif]]
> Repetition/variant of the previous pane.

> [!example]- 11 — "And last, was the girl. At last, was the girl."
> ![[11_and_last_was_the_girl_at_last_was_the_girl.gif]]
> The double line ("and last" / "at last") is the line that underpins the whole theory of whether the final "girl" is the same as the Second Hero's "girl," or a distinct third person — see the theory already documented in [[Susie]].

> [!example]- 12 — "The Second Hero. The Girl, with hope crossed on her heart."
> ![[12_the_second_hero_the_girl_with_hope_crossed_on_her_heart.gif]]
> Describes the **second** hero — "the monster" from the prose text — as a girl with "hope crossed on her heart." This is exactly the line the [[Susie]] note already uses as the basis for the theory "Is Susie the girl of the Last Prophecy?": the symbol accompanying this pane is the same Rude Buster that decorates her bedroom's rug.

> [!example]- 13 — "The Girl, with hope crossed on her heart."
> ![[13_the_girl_with_hope_crossed_on_her_heart_2.gif]]
> Repetition/variant of the previous pane.

### Block 3 — Omens of the Knight and its enemies (panes 14-19)

A block of paired couplets that announce, in very symbolic terms, the confrontations against the Roaring Knight and several already-known chapter antagonists.

> [!example]- 14 — "They'll hear the ring of Heaven's call."
> ![[14_theyll_hear_the_ring_of_heavens_call.gif]]
> Possible connection to the "Angel's Heaven" that must be banished according to the prose text — see [[Ángel]].

> [!example]- 15 — "They'll see the tail of Hell take crawl."
> ![[15_theyll_see_the_trail_of_hell_take_crawl.gif]]
> **Transcription correction**: the real word is "tail," not "trail" — the OCR originally used to build this section misread it the first time. With "tail" in play, the fandom draws a direct connection: "the Tail of Hell" is the popular nickname for [[FRIEND]], the feline entity that stalks the group from the shadows across several chapters — its internal filename in the game's files (`IMAGE_FRIEND`, `DEVICE_FRIEND`) and its design made of circles fit that image of a winding "tail." **Very widespread fan theory, not confirmed by name within the game.**

> [!example]- 16 — "The Knight which makes with blackened knife."
> ![[16_the_knight_which_makes_with_blackened_knife.gif]]
> Direct reference to the [[Roaring Knight]] and their weapon — matches the theories linking the Knight's "Black Knife" to [[Dess]] (see the Dess note, theory "Is Dess the Roaring Knight?").

> [!example]- 17 — "Shall duel with heroes strife by strife."
> ![[17_shall_duel_with_heroes_strife_by_strife.gif]]
> Paired with the previous pane — announces the progressive confrontations against the Knight across several chapters.

> [!example]- 18 — "The Queen's chariot cannot be stopped."
> ![[18_the_queens_chariot_cannot_be_stopped.gif]]
> **Confirmed**: not a speculative reading — the pane's own image literally shows [[Queen]] inside her Cyber World car, the same one seen driving through Castle Town in Ch.2. This pane also follows the same internal-file naming convention ("boss") as Tenna's and Asgore's (see panes 19 and 20), confirming each one portrays the main antagonist of its corresponding chapter.

> [!example]- 19 — "The Lord of Screens cleaved red by blade."
> ![[19_the_lord_of_screens_cleaved_red_by_blade.gif]]
> This is [[Tenna]], director of *TV Time* and ruler of the TV World — the literal "Lord of Screens." "Cleaved red by blade" fits the moment the Roaring Knight cuts off their arms after defeating them in Ch.3, leaving them bleeding. One of the most solid identifications in this whole block.

### Block 4 — Everyday winks and riddles (panes 20-24)

The tone shifts entirely: from grand battles to domestic, almost absurd predictions — the contrast itself is part of the joke.

> [!example]- 20 — "The Flower Man, trapped in asylum."
> ![[20_the_flower_man_trapped_in_asylum.gif]]
> **Confirmed**: it's [[Asgore]], shown in the pane itself with his hands around a single flower and, curiously, wearing a crown — something he doesn't have in the Light World. This pane's internal filename follows the same "boss" convention as Queen's and Tenna's (panes 18 and 19), confirming it portrays the main antagonist of its chapter — in this case, Ch.5 (Flowery/Flower Kingdom).
>
> "Asylum" is the piece that connects several things in the vault:
> - It's a synonym for **shelter** — which is why much of the fandom reads this line as a direct hint at [[Shelter]] itself, long before Ch.5 confirmed Asgore's actual relationship to that area.
> - It fits that Flower King — his own flower shop — is, in practice, his personal "asylum": the place where he's lived alone, tending the 7 Flowers well past their natural lifespan (see [[7 Flores de Colores|7 Colored Flowers]]), ever since he divorced Toriel.
> - It also connects to **Gerson's book**: [[Gerson Boom]] is the author of the novel *Lord of the Hammer*, directly inspired by this same Prophecy — and its chapter titles are an almost literal mirror of the actual chapters of the **video game itself** ("Chapter 4, The Trials of the Sacred Hammer"; "Chapter 5, The Field of Rose and Gold"). In other words: the Prophecy doesn't just predict Deltarune's events — within the fiction itself, there's already a book written by another character reinterpreting it chapter by chapter, almost like a Deltarune within Deltarune.

> [!example]- 21 — "Jockington grows the beard."
> ![[21_jockington_grows_the_beard.gif]]
> **This is the line that confirms the fact already noted in Quick facts**: Jockington is the only character mentioned by his own name in the entire Last Prophecy — and for something as mundane as growing a beard. See [[Jockington]].

> [!example]- 22 — "The pointy-headed will say \"toothpaste,\" and then \"boy.\""
> ![[22_the_pointy_headed_will_say_toothpaste_and_then_boy.gif]]
> Reasoned reading: "pointy-headed" fits [[Lancer]]'s design better than any other Darkner — there's even a loose sprite in the vault (`lancer_Darkner_face_profecy.png`) that seems to confirm this same visual association. **No direct textual confirmation.**

> [!example]- 23 — "A conundrum's first four tones."
> ![[23_a_conundrums_first_four_tones.gif]]

> [!example]- 24 — "A conundrum's last four tones."
> ![[24_a_conundrums_last_four_tones.gif]]
> Paired with the previous pane — probably a reference to some minigame or musical puzzle within Ch.4 itself, not identified with certainty.

### Block 5 — The Last Prophecy: the ending (panes 25-31)

The tone shifts again, now toward the climax — the block that gives the "Last Prophecy" its own name as a distinct section within the whole.

> [!example]- 25 — "The Last Prophecy. The Story's End."
> ![[25_the_last_prophecy_the_storys_end.gif]]
> Opening title of this second block — it's, literally, the pane that gives this note its name.

> [!example]- 26 — "And then, when all hope is lost for the tale"
> ![[26_and_then_when_all_hope_is_lost_for_the_tale.gif]]

> [!example]- 27 — "The final tragedy unveils."
> ![[27_the_final_tragedy_unveils.gif]]
> Paired with the previous pane.

> [!example]- 28 — "To save the worlds, there is only one way."
> ![[28_to_save_the_worlds_there_is_only_one_way.gif]]

> [!example]- 29 — "Only then, will the worlds be saved."
> ![[29_only_then_will_the_worlds_be_saved.gif]]
> Paired with the previous pane.

> [!example]- 30 — "Axe carved by the tortoise's grand hammer."
> ![[30_axe_carved_by_the_tortoises_grand_hammer.gif]]
> **Confirmed by design**: "the tortoise" is [[Gerson Boom]] — he's literally a turtle, and his weapon as the Hammer of Justice is, precisely, an axe/hammer. There's even a sprite dedicated to this pane in the vault (`gerson_3rd_Sanctuary_location_prophecy_axe.png`). One of the most solid connections in this whole analysis.

> [!example]- 31 — "Coldwater fell from the top of the sky."
> ![[31_goldwater_fell_from_the_top_of_the_sky.gif]]
> **Transcription correction**: the real word is "Coldwater," not "Goldwater" — confirmed by Toby Fox himself, who paraphrased this pane (one that was at one point cut/unused) in an official newsletter as "Cold water falls from the top of the sky." No clear identification of which specific event within the plot it refers to.

### Block 6 — Epilogue (panes 32-35)

The final block, much shorter and more intimate in tone than the rest — closes the sequence by focusing on "the girl" rather than on any conflict.

> [!example]- 32 — "Roots"
> ![[32_roots_raices.gif]]
> A short pane, but with a lot behind it. The image shows a house between two trees — the same pair of trees that can be made out in the background of pane 05 ("The Light and Dark, both burning"), which has led part of the fandom to connect the concept of "roots" to the Holiday house or to [[Shelter]] itself.
>
> "Roots" isn't exclusive to this pane: it's also the title of two soundtrack pieces ("Digital Roots," which plays in Queen's Mansion basement in Ch.2, and "BIT ROOTS," which plays in MANTLE's final dungeon in Ch.3+4), and one of the possible words in Bibliox's "spelling correction" minigame in Ch.4. It had already been hinted at months before Ch.4's release, on the `/lostwheretheforestwouldgrow/` page of the Spamton Sweepstakes — the URL's own name ("where the lost forest would grow") fits the image of the two trees. Toby Fox has even hinted that "Roots" might also be the title of a still-unreleased track for Ch.6 or Ch.7.
>
> Right at the end of the stained-glass hallway that ends on this one ("Roots"), an Organikk (one of the furniture-enemies in the Sanctuary) drops a hint about something being "in between" — the same kind of hint that in earlier chapters pointed to where to find the chapter's secret Egg. **"Roots"'s ultimate meaning is still never explained within the game — this is a description of where and how it appears, not a closed interpretation.**

> [!example]- 33 — "Gallery"
> ![[33_gallery_galeria.gif]]
> This is, literally, the name of the stained-glass hallway itself: a very long corridor full of crystal panes, located just to the right of the "Love finds its way to the girl" pane (34). The Ch.4 secret Egg is hidden there — you have to notice which window "looks different" from the rest and enter through it — and, in the upper-left corner of that same room, there's a "peculiar" book you can read for additional hints about the background of Deltarune's world.
>
> Through its exit, the Gallery connects directly to [[Gerson Boom]]'s study — the same Gerson who authored *Lord of the Hammer*, the novel inspired by this Prophecy (see pane 20, above). The physical closeness between the Gallery, its mysterious book, and the study of the only character in the game known for writing fiction based on the Prophecy doesn't seem like a coincidence, though the game never explicitly confirms the Gallery's book is his work.

> [!example]- 34 — "Love finds its way to the girl."
> ![[34_love_finds_its_way_to_the_girl.gif]]
> Returns focus to "the girl" from the three-heroes block — reinforces the reading that her personal story (possibly [[Susie]]'s with [[Noelle]]) is the emotional close chosen for the whole sequence, above the conflict with the Knight itself.

> [!example]- 35 — "The Girl"
> ![[35_the_girl_final.gif]]
> Close of the sequence, focused entirely on her.

## Theories

> [!question]+ Who really wrote the Prophecy?
> The textual match with Gaster's Entry 17 has led part of the fandom to wonder whether the Prophecy itself was written, inspired, or altered by the same entity behind the Voice. A strong theory given the near-literal quote, but without direct confirmation within the game itself.

> [!question]- Why is Jockington the only one named?
> Of the entire legend, Jockington is the only character explicitly mentioned by their own name — neither the Knight nor Queen are. There's no official explanation for why he specifically was chosen as the only named reference within the prophetic text.
