*Note: unlike Spamton, Tenna does have a clear, recognizable "Light World" form — it's literally the TV in the Dreemurrs' living room, with screen burn-in implied by long disuse.*

> [!info]+ Quick facts
> 📺 Director of *TV Time* in the TV World; main antagonist and second-to-last boss of Chapter 3.
> 👁️ A tall, thin Darkner with a light-purple CRT television head, eyeless, with two round-tipped antennas and a long, detachable nose he keeps spares of in a "nose drawer."
> 🌸 A pink flower can sprout from the tip of his nose when he's genuinely happy; when his screen goes blank or turns to static, the nose disappears.
> 📏 He can shrink, grow, or flatten depending on his mood — the same effect affects his antennas, his tie, and his coattails.
> 🔒 He secretly kept Toriel locked in a gachapon capsule, being the last member of the Dreemurr family to see him turned on.
> 🤝 He has a hidden pact with Kris, unwittingly revealed on the Sword Route.
> 💼 Spamton's former business partner, now his archenemy — although Ch.5 reveals he really did care about him.
> 😱 Behind his enthusiasm lies a deep fear that the Lightners will abandon him for good — the real reason behind almost all of his harmful decisions.

> [!tip]- Personality profile
> The archetype of a TV host: energetic, enthusiastic, always ready with a quip, theatrical to the extreme, and with an absurd, madcap sense of humor. Being an old CRT, he's quite out of touch — he doesn't really understand email, believes cryptocurrency is trustworthy, and considers his retro video game "The Legacy of Tenna" to be "cutting-edge." He's nostalgic for the days when the Dreemurr and Holiday families used to gather around him. Beneath his cheerful facade he hides a deep fear of abandonment: that insecurity is what led him to convince Spamton to sign a contract he knew would hurt him, and later made him strike a deal with the Roaring Knight to keep several Lightners captive, including two of his former owners. He also resorts to shady business practices with his employees: fine-print contracts, misleading compensation, and veiled threats regarding pay.

> [!tip]- Speech pattern
> In normal dialogue (not in battle), his text is accompanied by "Word Art" — stylized logos embedded within his sentences, internally called `funnytext` — inspired by the character P.T. Bridgeport from the *Pogo* comic strips. In the Japanese version he has an additional verbal tic: he replaces the future-tense ending しょう (*shou*) with "SHOW" in Latin characters. He shares that same kind of verbal tic with Spamton, who in Japanese replaces です with "DEATH" and だい with "DIE."

## Story by chapter

> [!example]+ Background — The living room TV
> Tenna is the CRT television in the Dreemurr household's living room, the one that entertained both the Dreemurrs and the Holidays. Asriel and Kris used to watch cartoons while playing, Toriel watched cooking shows, and both families always gathered around him during the holiday specials.
>
> ![[tenna_with_dreemur_and_holiday.webp]]
> *The Dreemurr and Holiday families gathered in front of Tenna in his golden age.*
>
> In the Light World, Kris and a girl once brought a laptop home to play doctors and mothers — according to Ramb, those pretend games involved Queen, King, and Tenna himself. In the Dark World, the arrival of that laptop is what caused Tenna and Spamton to meet and become business partners during the latter's period of success.
>
> ![[tenna_history_spamton.webp]]
> *Tenna and Spamton in their collaborative days, before everything that would follow.*
>
> Over time, both families watched the TV less and less. Toriel was the last to watch him before he was unplugged for a long stretch — dusty, unused, and possibly with screen burn-in. Toriel even considered throwing him out.

> [!example]+ Chapter 2 — Mentioned by Spamton
> Tenna doesn't appear yet, but he's already mentioned indirectly: if you choose "FRIENDS" when talking to Spamton in his shop, he rants about not needing friends, including "CRTs," and warns Kris not to trust anything they see on TV — a direct reference to Tenna.

> [!example]+ Chapter 3 — Director of TV Time

He interrupts Susie and Ralsei's conversation on the Couch Cliffs, praising them, and physically appears after the VHS tape that Mike plays.

Meanwhile, in the Green Room, he holds secret phone calls with his subordinates about something he wants to hide from the group — Susie suspects something, and upon investigating they discover he had Toriel locked in a gachapon capsule, calling her his "ace up his sleeve" [TODO: verify exact EN quote] for being the one who cared for him the most.

> Since the group refuses to keep playing, Tenna locks them in capsules too and forces them to take part in the "bonus round," growing more and more unstable out of fear that the Lightners will leave. When they finally escape, he orders his employees to search for them — and, after failing over and over, he breaks down and pushes everyone away until they abandon him, with Mike being the last to go.
>
> ![[tenna_villian.gif]]
> *Tenna as main antagonist, already on the verge of collapse.*
>
> Upon returning from the TV World, Tenna blocks the path to the Dark Fountain and explains his motive: he was the Dreemurrs' beloved TV, and was gradually abandoned; after the Roaring Knight appeared, he accepted a deal to keep the group occupied in order to feel relevant again. Seeing that he can't convince them, he starts a battle as a final challenge that lasts until enough points are accumulated.
>
> Upon being defeated, he admits he just wanted to be watched. Ralsei tries to comfort him by telling him he's already fulfilled his purpose; Susie steps in and tells him her own story to cheer him up: when she arrived in Hometown she had no friends and felt very alone, until one day, crying on a bench in the cemetery, Toriel approached her, gave her something to eat, and told her everything would be okay and that she would soon find friends. With that in mind, Susie promises Tenna that she and Kris will find someone to watch him again. Moved, Tenna agrees to free Toriel — but right after, the Knight slices off his arms as punishment, dealing between 5,500 and 5,999 damage and leaving him out of commission. He doesn't appear again for the rest of the chapter.

## Theories

> [!question]- The title "LORD OF SCREENS" in the Prophecy
> Tenna is mentioned in the Prophecy tapestries under the title "LORD OF SCREENS" — one of the few Darkners, along with the Knight, referred to by a title rather than a proper name within the prophetic text. [[Profecía|Prophecy]]
>
> ![[tenna_profecy.webp]]
> *The fragment of the Prophecy that refers to Tenna as "Lord of Screens."*

> [!question]- Everything hidden in the introduction VHS
> The VHS video with which Tenna introduces himself ("Mr. (Ant) Tenna's TV Time!") isn't just a simple bumper — it's an actual video file within the game, and several loose details within it have generated their own theories.
>
> ![[tenna_introduction.webp]]
> *The most-replayed frame of the introduction VHS.*
>
> **The egg and the Pipis.** At one point in the video a Pipis appears (the same blue creature Tenna has kept since his falling-out with Spamton) being shot at repeatedly, accompanied by the slogan "You can't get this from an EGG!" It's the Pipis's first appearance in the whole chapter, even before Tenna himself mentions it in person — and it comes wrapped in an '80s TV-commercial-style joke, not presented as a serious clue.
>
> **The blue silhouette in "repair mode."** A community reading (first documented by Tumblr user katy-133) notes that, alongside Tenna's red silhouette moving fluidly through the VHS, there's a second Tenna in a blue silhouette that moves stiffly, almost like a cardboard cutout — a simple "placeholder" instead of a full animation. The theory proposes that the video was originally recorded to introduce Tenna **and** Spamton together (hence the blue egg right before it, already associated with Spamton's color), and that after the two broke up, Tenna re-recorded his own silhouette on top without fully replacing his former partner's, leaving that "gap" half-fixed. The syllable count of the slogan itself would fit the theory: "Mr.-Ant-Ten-na's" has the same number of syllables as "Spam-ton-and-Ten-na's."
>
> **The split screen.** In a very brief instant of the same VHS, the TV logo is shown split in two ("cleaved") — an image that shouldn't have any narrative reason to appear for Tenna, since he doesn't seem to know anything about the Prophecy or his own role in it. The same fandom reading suggests that, if it was Spamton who had a hand in editing the original video, and Spamton does know (at least in part) his role in the Prophecy, that detail could be a leftover trace of his slipped into the edit without Tenna himself understanding it.
>
> **Direct nods to Spamton in later dialogue.** Once outside the VHS, Tenna drops lines like "COWABUNGA-DERO! THAT'S THE SMOOTH TASTE OF TV TIME!" and "Fresh from the Juice, Fresh from the Juice! Make sure you don't get it on your shoese!" — direct echoes of Spamton's vocabulary ("cowabungadero," "fresh"). If you're also carrying the PuppetScarf or the Dealmaker in your inventory and haven't completed the Weird Route in Ch.2, a secret interaction in the Cold Place reveals that Tenna still keeps the Pipis in a closet because of his former partner — and if Spamton appears for an instant, Tenna reacts with genuine panic, insists he doesn't recognize him, sprays him with foam, and runs out of the room. **Everything above is drawn from real text and design clues; the reading that the VHS was literally edited by or for Spamton is fandom interpretation, with no direct textual confirmation from Toby Fox.**

## Trivia
- Toby Fox voices Tenna as well as Spamton, Jevil, Queen, and [[Flowery]].
- He's one of the few Darkners whose design is based directly on an object from the Dreemurrs' own living room (the television) rather than an object from the closet or the school.
- His design is inspired by TV Dinnah, from *Little King's Story* — confirmed by Toby Fox himself, who also worked in a nod to Yoshiro Kimura (that game's director and a friend of his) through a Papyrus cameo in Undertale.
- He's also inspired by Bob Sparker, from the web novel *Tales from Electricopolis* — the same character who already influenced Mettaton, from Undertale.
- "Ant Tenna" comes from "antenna," a leftover from an earlier design phase in which Tenna looked more like an ant.
- During the 2022 Spamton Sweepstakes, a hidden page showed the Dreemurrs' TV crossed out with a red X, with Spamton calling Tenna a "boob tube" [TODO: verify exact EN quote] — the first confirmation that the TV entity was Tenna, and that Tenna and Mike are separate characters.
