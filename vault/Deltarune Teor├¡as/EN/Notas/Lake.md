> [!info]+ Quick facts
> 🌊 Hometown location that's practically decorative during Ch.1-3 — no events of its own, just background scenery.
> 🐙 Onionsan, the Lake's recurring NPC, disappears starting in Ch.4 — coinciding with the Normal NPC's first appearance in the same spot, in the middle of a downpour.
> 🌀 It's the setting of the Ch.4 Weird Route (where it starts changing color and sound) and culminates in the Ch.5 one, where Kris and Noelle end up submerging beneath the surface.
> 💿 The theme for that scene is officially called "The Other Side of the Lake" — the name itself confirms that there is, narratively, an "other side" the game is pointing directly at.
> 🎣 In the Ch.5 normal route, it's the setting for [[Susie]] and Noelle's date after the Festival — [[Carol]] calls twice, and Noelle only manages to gather the strength to reject the second call.
> 📀 If you keep pressing "Continue" all the way through the end of the Weird Route, the screen cuts to "Insert Chapter 7, Side B" and generates a special save file with the "_b" suffix — not a normal completion FILE.

## Chapter-by-chapter history

> [!example]+ Chapters 1-3 — Just decoration, but with an early clue
> The Lake exists as part of Hometown's scenery from Ch.1 onward, with no plot events of its own — in practice, it's just background. Its only fixed resident during this stage is Onionsan, who does have dialogue of his own: in Ch.1 he only shyly asks [TODO: verify exact EN quote] "Do you want to be my friend?", but in Ch.2 he drops a line that will turn out to be much more important in hindsight: [TODO: verify exact EN quote] "Sometimes... I hear a song, at night." — the series' very first hint about the Lake's "song," long before Susie mentions it in Ch.4 or the Ch.5 Weird Route develops it fully.

> > [!danger]- Ch.5 Weird Route — the full scene
> > It's only accessible if the Ch.4 Weird Route has already been completed. At the start of the chapter, you have to force Kris out of bed by repeatedly pressing directions, leave the house, and go with Susie to Sans's ice cream stand — if you don't stop by there first, Susie mentions that Noelle wanted ice cream and asks Kris to help her find some. From there, they walk together to the Lake; once they arrive, Susie waves goodbye and Kris automatically enters the Lake room alone — from that point on, you can no longer leave the room.
> >
> > Noelle begins a monologue about how limited her life felt after Dess's disappearance, and how Kris "changed" and "figured out how to stop being Kris" — before asking him to help her do "something crazy": cross to the other side of the Lake and leave Hometown forever. From there, **13 consecutive choices** appear between "Stop" and "Continue". For the first 6, it doesn't matter which one you pick — the scene proceeds the same either way — but the game does internally track how many times "Stop" was chosen (a hidden variable that affects small later details of the normal playthrough). If you keep choosing "Stop", Noelle eventually flies into a rage, reproaching Kris that his voice "doesn't sound the same as before" and that he doesn't even know how to "tell me to stop like he used to" — and ends up forcing every option to "Continue" herself.
> >

> > From there, Kris and Noelle crawl together into the water, moving away from the shore — the screen's color grows increasingly saturated as they advance, and **every press of "Continue" briefly resets the colors to normal** before they intensify again, an effect that physically conveys a feeling of going "farther and farther, and grayer/more saturated" with every step. They try to keep their heads above water without succeeding, sinking further and further while Noelle begs Kris to keep telling her to continue. If you keep pressing "Continue" even once the screen fades to white and only the SOUL remains visible, the game goes silent for several seconds before cutting to a screen with a CRT effect and the text "Insert Chapter 7, Side B" — generating a special save file with the "_b" suffix instead of a normal completion FILE, and returning the player to the chapter select screen with no save to load.
> >
> > If instead you let the screen fade to white without continuing to press "Continue" (or if you're not fast enough), Kris wakes back up on the usual bench and Ch.5 continues normally, with small dialogue changes — implying that everything before could have been, at least in part, a fantasy or a failed attempt, not an accomplished fact within that playthrough.

## Theories

> [!question]+ What really happened to Onionsan?
> He disappears right when the Normal NPC starts occupying his spot in Ch.4, and a Pipis mentions that the takoyaki the Normal NPC sells [TODO: verify exact EN quote] "tastes like raw onion" — a fairly direct insinuation, though never explicitly confirmed, that Onionsan himself might have ended up turned into an ingredient. **Widely spread fan theory, backed by a real line of dialogue but without direct textual confirmation.**
>
> ![[Normal_NPC_screenshot_lake.png]]
> *The Normal NPC, already occupying the spot where Onionsan used to be — the visual basis for this theory.*

> [!question]+ "Farther and farther, grayer" — color as a danger meter
> During the submersion in the Ch.5 Weird Route, the screen progressively saturates/darkens the further you advance toward the center of the Lake, with every press of "Continue" briefly resetting the colors to normal before they intensify again. The visual effect functions almost like a meter of "how much is left before the point of no return" — the farther from shore, the more the scene's real color is lost. **Description of a visual effect confirmed within the game itself; the reading that it represents a symbolic "meter" is interpretation.**
>
> ![[Lake_more_grey.png]]
> *The progressive desaturation effect during the submersion — the scene loses color the farther it gets from shore.*

> [!question]- What's on the other side / beneath the surface?
> The biggest loose end in the series to date. The scene's official theme name itself, "The Other Side of the Lake", confirms that a destination on the other side narratively exists — matching the theory that Ch.7's "Side B" takes place precisely there. It isn't known whether there's a Dark Fountain hidden under the water, nor how this connects to Dess's disappearance. The community speculates with all kinds of theories, none confirmed.
>
> ![[lake_noelle_and_kriss_drowning.png]]
> *Kris and Noelle, already submerged beneath the surface — the exact moment the game leaves open the question of what's on the other side.*
>
> 🎲 A 2026 reading (see [[Roaring Knight]]) points out that the never-reached strip on the far side of the Lake is shown in white — an unusual color for a map boundary, which are normally black — and compares it to the Underground barrier in Undertale, which Alphys explains requires at least one human soul and one monster soul to cross. Under that reading, the Lake would have the same kind of barrier, and crossing it would require a similar combination of souls. It's a striking hypothesis but built on the color of a map boundary, with no line of dialogue confirming it within Deltarune itself — very speculative on its own.

> [!question]+ The song that comes from afar — Susie, Gerson, the Riverman, and Gaster
> Several scattered pieces, spread throughout the game (and through Undertale), suggest that the Lake's "song" isn't an isolated element, but part of one single musical thread running through the whole saga:
> - **Onionsan** already mentions in Ch.2 that [TODO: verify exact EN quote] "sometimes he hears a song, at night" (see above) — the series' very first clue.
> - **Susie** can hear part of that same music from afar even while staying outside the Lake room in Ch.5 (see the "important detail" for Ch.5), and she also shares with Kris a real, confirmed theme that plays specifically if both of them sit together on the Lake's shore in Ch.2 — the same theme plays again if Kris stays still at the end of the Lake path in Ch.2 and Ch.4.
> - **Gerson Boom**, upon being revealed as the Ch.4 secret boss, literally asks: [TODO: verify exact EN quote] *"Can you hear it? Can you hear its song, singing from the depths...? That... is the toll... of the Hammer of Justice."* — the line most cited by those who connect all of this, for how directly it mentions a song "from the depths."
> - Undertale's **Riverman** (internal file name: `spr_riverman`) sings nonstop while rowing through Waterfall, and among his random lines drops two of the most-cited warnings in the whole game: [TODO: verify exact EN quote] *"Beware of the man who speaks in hands"* (about Gaster) and [TODO: verify exact EN quote] *"Beware of the man who came from another world"* — the latter, written years before Deltarune existed, now reads as an almost literal preview that Sans does, in fact, come from another world.
> - The very water sound used on Deltarune's official website (`water.ogg`) is, technically, the same echo variant already used in certain areas of Waterfall and in the Riverman's rooms in Undertale — a real sound-file link, not just a thematic one.
> - Undertale also has a secret, nearly impossible to find "Sound Room" (only accessible with a very specific internal "Fun" value), with four songs never used in the game — one of them literally titled "Gaster's Theme". That same melody shares a musical motif with themes that play at the beginning and end of Deltarune.
> - 🎲 According to a 2026 theory (see [[Roaring Knight]]), approaching the Lake barrier at the end of the Ch.2 Weird Route (Snowgrave) plays a modified version of the music that accompanies the fight against Endogeny, one of Undertale's True Lab amalgams — a possible additional strand in this same musical thread, though for now unconfirmed against the game's actual audio files.
>
> Put all together, the fandom's reading is that the "song from the depths" Gerson mentions, the one Onionsan hears at night, the one Susie and Kris share at the Lake, and the Riverman himself singing in Undertale, could be variations of one same background melody associated with Gaster — present in both games long before anyone knew they were connected. **A very dense fan theory with a fair amount of real file-based support (sound clues, internal names, shared leitmotifs), but the conclusion that it all forms one single, deliberate musical thread remains interpretation, not a direct statement from Toby Fox.**

## Trivia
- It's the only place in the game, to date, where completing a route generates a save file with a different suffix ("_b") instead of a normal completion FILE.
- The submersion scene's theme is officially titled "The Other Side of the Lake" — one of the few theme titles that so directly confirms a scene's own narrative destination.
- The Ch.5 Weird Route is only accessible if the Ch.4 one has already been completed — the Lake is, in that sense, the point where both chained hidden routes converge.
- The game's own main menu uses a water background image very similar in palette and texture to the Lake itself — part of the fandom reads this as a deliberate visual clue, though water is a recurring motif across several of the game's backgrounds and not exclusive to this area.
