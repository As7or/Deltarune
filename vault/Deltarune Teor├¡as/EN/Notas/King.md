> [!info]+ Quick facts
> 👑 Lancer's father and the main antagonist of Ch.1, guardian of the Eastern Fountain atop Card Castle.
> 🗡️ Proclaimed himself absolute ruler of the Kingdom alongside Lancer after the Roaring Knight's arrival, locking up the other three kings (hearts, diamonds, clubs) in the castle.
> 😠 Hates the Lightners, convinced they abandoned him; believes he no longer needs them to have a purpose, since he now serves the Roaring Knight.
> 🏰 His ultimate goal is to cover the entire world in darkness so the Darkners can rule over it.
> 👊 Uses Lancer as a hostage to try to force the party's surrender, threatening to throw him off the castle roof.
> 🃏 He has never actually met the Roaring Knight in person — everything he knows about him was told to him by Jevil, his former court jester, before Jevil fell into madness.
> 💔 Ch.5 reveals the real origin of his tragedy: his physical card was accidentally torn when a Lightner's hand slipped while they were playing — the incident that led to the whole deck being discarded and left forgotten in an abandoned classroom. That tear manifests in the Dark World as the scar on his cheek, which he now shows off with pride, even though he still blames himself for his people's abandonment.
>

> [!tip]- Personality profile
> A tyrant, spiteful and deeply insecure beneath his villain facade — his hatred of the Lightners stems from a genuine feeling of abandonment. Despite his cruelty, something in him still loves Lancer: his threat to throw him off the roof was never more than a bluff, something he himself confirms after his defeat.

## Chapter-by-chapter history

> [!example]+ Chapter 1 — The tyrant of Card Castle
> He guards the Eastern Fountain at the top of the castle. When the party arrives, he uses Lancer as a hostage to force their surrender, threatening to throw him off the roof — but Lancer manages to attack him from behind and escape, leading into the chapter's final battle.
>
> During the fight, he rants about his hatred of the Lightners, whom he accuses of having abandoned the Darkners, and insists he no longer needs them to feel he has a purpose, since he now serves the Roaring Knight. His stated goal is to cover the entire world in darkness under Darkner rule. After being defeated, he surrenders and "admits" that he never actually wanted to go to war against the Lightners.
>

> [!example]+ Chapter 2 — A distant father
> It's revealed that he spends very little time with Lancer and barely worries about his well-being, leaving his day-to-day care in the hands of Rouxls Kaard. Even so, he does care how Lancer is treated in public, forcing other Darkners to obey his son's orders even though he doesn't want to. Talking to him again reveals that his threat to throw Lancer off the roof in Ch.1 was only a bluff he'd never have gone through with — by his own account, it wouldn't even have mattered, since Lancer would simply have [TODO: verify exact EN quote] "bounced like a little ball" on the way down. He fondly calls him [TODO: verify exact EN quote] "my little jumping pumpkin." After Queen's defeat, she visits him in his cell, declaring that from now on she'll take care of Lancer; the two call each other pet names ("Kingy Wingy" / "Queenie Beanie"), and King gives her parenting advice: if Lancer cries, just bounce him like a ball to calm him down.

> [!example]+ Chapter 3 — a childhood game of pretend
> Ramb nostalgically reveals that Kris and Noelle used to play as kids with King and Queen's Light World objects — a pretend royal couple, [TODO: verify exact EN quote] "Queen on one side, King on the other, Tenna behind" — even taking a laptop home just so they could play house with them. It's the most direct proof that Kris's connection to the Card Kingdom goes back long before Ch.1.

> [!example]+ Chapter 4 — Jevil, Seam, and a new jester
> Now imprisoned, he offers new dialogue if asked about the "Jester": he confesses he never actually met the Roaring Knight in person, and that everything he knows was told to him by Jevil before Jevil's descent into madness. He speaks of him with unexpected fondness: [TODO: verify exact EN quote] *"It was he, the clown, who told me of the Knight's brilliance... of the glory of the fountains, and of how I would come to power. A funny little man. How his jokes and his dances made us laugh. A shame he had to be deceived and cast aside by his closest companion, the magician Seam, whose smile has been gone ever since that day."* — showing sympathy both for Jevil and for Seam's own anguish at having to lock up his friend.
>
> If Tenna hasn't been taken to Mettaton, he can present himself to King and Lancer as their new court jester; King is initially unimpressed by his jokes, but after watching him comically trip, he admits, laughing along with Lancer, that [TODO: verify exact EN quote] "he likes this one."
>
> Queen also visits him in his cell during Ch.4, and in front of the party the two of them openly affirm that they were together and that Lancer is their son — the most direct confirmation in the whole game of Lancer's origin, even more explicit than the "ex-husband, or whatever he is" line from Ch.5.
>
> ![[queen_king_interaction.png]]

> [!example]+ Chapter 5 — the scar and the real origin of the abandonment
> The concrete origin of his tragedy is finally revealed: his physical card was accidentally torn when a Lightner's hand slipped while they were playing with it, which led to the whole deck being discarded and the Card Kingdom ending up forgotten in an unused classroom. That same tear manifests in the Dark World as the visible scar on his cheek — which he now openly shows off, though deep down he still feels guilty that his people were abandoned because of him.
>
> Imprisoned, he speaks of himself almost in the register of legend: *"A single scarred king... that rendered the whole kingdom..."* [TODO: verify full quote] — his own, more poetic way of summing up that same tragedy in front of the party.

## Relationships

> [!quote]- Lancer
> His son, whom he treats with indifference and uses as a hostage at the climax of Ch.1 — but whom, deep down, he would never truly have hurt. [[Lancer]]

> [!quote]- Roaring Knight
> His new "purpose" after the Knight's arrival: he claims to serve him directly, and it's the Knight who pushes him toward villainy and self-proclaimed absolute rule over the Kingdom. [[Roaring Knight]]

> [!quote]- Jevil
> Before his imprisonment, it was Jevil who told him about the Roaring Knight's glory — the spark that ignited King's villainy. In Ch.4, he speaks of him with fondness and respect despite everything that happened. [[Jevil]]

In Ch.4, the two of them affirm to the party that they were together and that Lancer is their son; in Ch.5 she also openly calls him her "ex-husband, or whatever he is." [[Queen]]

> [!quote]- Kris and Noelle
> Ramb reveals in Ch.3 that as kids they played house with King and Queen's Light World objects, even taking a whole laptop home just for that. [[Kris]] [[Noelle]]

> [!quote]- Seam
> It was Seam who had to lock up Jevil, his closest friend, after his fall into madness — King acknowledges in Ch.4 the anguish that must have caused him. [[Seam]]

## Trivia
- Jevil, his former court jester, was the one who told him about the glory of the Roaring Knight and the Fountain — the spark that ignited his ambition and villainy, even though King has never actually met the Knight in person.
- He is one of the few Deltarune bosses whose defeat includes such a direct "confession" about not having wanted the war.
