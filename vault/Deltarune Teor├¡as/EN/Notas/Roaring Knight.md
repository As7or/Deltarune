*Unlike the rest of the characters in this folder, the Knight is not a Darkner — Susie confirms he's a Lightner under a mask, so he has no "Dark World" form of his own, just a still-unrevealed Light World identity (see Theories).*

> [!info]+ Quick facts
> 🗡️ Masked figure in the service of "The Roaring," central to the Prophecy that foretells his duel against the heroes. He's officially called only "Knight" on the wiki.
> 👤 Susie directly confirms, after seeing him at night in the Light World, that **he's a Lightner** — ruling out a native Darkner and reinforcing that he's someone from town under a mask.
> 👑 The King of Cards, driven by resentment over having been abandoned and forgotten in a disused classroom, is the one who receives from his jester Jevil the idea of the Knight's "genius" and a way to rise to power — the Knight, then, inspires other antagonists from earlier chapters without having acted himself yet.
> 🎯 He has tried, on orders, to kidnap Toriel (Ch.3, failed → kidnaps Undyne instead, also in Ch.3) and Asgore (Ch.5, successful) — a pattern of three attempts on the Dreemurr family/their close circle.
> 🤝 He has attack patterns specifically designed to not damage (or damage much less) Kris, while knocking out Ralsei and Susie normally — a clear mechanical clue, separate from the question of his identity, suggesting Kris cooperates or has some kind of understanding with him.
> ⚔️ When defeated in Ch.3, his "knighting" pose over Kris has the internally confirmed name "Nighting" in the game's files — not just a fandom visual reading.

> [!tip]- Personality profile
> He has almost no dialogue or directly expressed personality — he communicates mostly through his actions: methodical, silent, and absolutely determined when pursuing his specific goals. His treatment of Kris — avoiding harming him in combat, going as far as "knighting" him after defeating him — contrasts sharply with the cold violence he shows toward the rest of the group and his kidnapping victims.

## Theories

> [!question]+ A deliberate pattern, or coincidence?
> Three attempts, three members of the Dreemurr family/their close circle (Toriel, Undyne, Asgore). Community analysis wonders whether the Knight is trying to silence someone who "knows too much," or whether these kidnappings are actually proof that the Knight has specifically targeted the Dreemurr family from the start.

> [!question]- Dess (the dominant theory about his identity)
> Silhouette compatible with Dess; he appears right in the chapters carrying the most emotional weight for the Holiday family. This is, by far, the reading with the most community consensus.
>
> ![[dess_knight1.png]]
> ![[dess_knight2.png]]
> ![[dess_knight3.png]]
> *Visual comparisons the fandom usually cites to back this theory: the antlers, the bat-like weapon, and the stigmata.*

> [!question]- Asriel (a real alternative theory, not just vague speculation)
> It has concrete evidence behind it, though the community considers it weaker than the Dess theory:
> - In an unused concept, the Knight's weapon is read as a "wiffle bat"-type bat with 40 attack — matching a cut Undertale item literally called "Asriel's Bat."
>
> ![[Knight_battle_intro_with_a_bat.gif]]
> *The Knight's battle-intro animation holding what looks like a wiffle-ball-type bat — the visual basis for this theory.*
>
> - The Knight shares an attack pattern and combat poses very similar to Kris's.
> - On one of Tenna's boards (Ch.3), it's revealed that Asriel's favorite character in a fighting video game is "Sword Magician" — the same type of character that attacks with swords, the Knight's signature weapon.
> - Mechanically, several of the Knight's attacks are programmed to **avoid or hit Kris specifically softer**, while they do knock out Ralsei and Susie — a design clue that fits with Kris recognizing (or directly working with) whoever is under the mask.
> - Against it: the "horns" on the helmet look more like the Holiday family's antlers than a Dreemurr's goat horns, and repeating the "the villain is actually a Dreemurr" twist would be an almost literal copy of Asriel/Flowey himself from Undertale — many fans see this as a strong reason to doubt this theory.
> - Additional weak argument (cited by community analysis, not very reliable): Dess is left-handed — according to objects in her room — and Asriel also happens to seem left-handed, judging by the position of his mouse pad in the room he shares with Kris. A curious detail, but one that doesn't prove anything by itself.
> - Alternative reading of those same "horns": if the helmet doesn't reflect what's underneath, but instead — as happens to other Lightners entering a Dark World (Toriel's nightcap turning into a crown, Berdly's glasses turning into a visor) — takes on a shape molded by the wearer's desires, Holiday-type antlers would fit an Asriel desperate to find Dess just as well as they'd fit Dess herself.
> - Narrative argument (an authorial reading, not textual): Toby Fox has described Deltarune as a story in dialogue with Undertale without being a simple repeat of it. Under that lens, the hooded villain turning out to be Asriel — the same character who in Undertale was already "the unexpected villain" — would work as a deliberate redemption of the character, giving him this time an emotional motivation (finding the one he wanted) instead of Flowey's lack of a soul. This is a reading of authorial intent, not a textual clue, so its weight depends on how much you trust the Deltarune/Undertale parallel as a deliberate narrative structure.
> - Staging argument (fandom observation, not confirmed): the Knight shares some pose gestures with Kris — pointing, or standing with his back turned and arms crossed — which would fit with Kris having learned those gestures as a kid by imitating his older brother, the same way he asked for a headband with horns as a child to look more like his adoptive family.
>
> *Sourcing note: the Knight's weapon read as a bat is already confirmed indirectly — during Undertale's 10th anniversary stream (Sept. 2025), Toby Fox showed an early design document confirming that a cut item literally called "Asriel's Bat" existed (alongside "Asriel's Shirt"). This isn't confirmation that the Knight uses that specific bat, but it does confirm that the bat as a weapon associated with Asriel is real, not just fandom speculation.*
>

> ![[asriel_roaringknight2.webp]]
> *Comparisons the fandom usually cites to back the theory that the Knight is Asriel.*

> [!question]- [[Carol]] and Rudy Holiday (weaker speculation, but worth considering)
> There's a minority branch of the Asriel theory that also imagines him secretly working with **Carol** to search for Dess on their own — which would explain why Carol investigates the Shelter area so insistently. It's an interesting idea but one that rests on a single thread of community speculation, with no direct textual clue from the game.
>
> ![[Carol_face_ominous_unused.png|120]]
> *A discarded portrait of Carol with a much more unsettling expression than usual — the design clue the fandom most often cites in favor of this variant. Full profile in [[Carol]].*
>
> The same originally applies to the variant pointing at **Rudy**, though in his case it has grown into its own candidacy for the Knight's identity, with considerably more development and weight than Carol's — not just a variant of the Asriel theory:
> - **Clear motivation**: if the Knight is trying to create Dark Fountains to recover someone lost, Rudy shares that exact motivation with Dess — the same as Carol, but without the suspicion weighing on her from her public role as mayor.
> - **The Holiday link**: the Knight has antlers, and so far the only consistent design trait he shares with the family is precisely that — deer antlers, the Holidays' species.
> - **Reacts warily to Susie in the Weird Route of Ch.4** — he asks Kris, annoyed, why he brought her, while he stays his usual self with Kris himself; a selective distrust toward someone who, not being part of "before," never fully got to know him.
> - **Geographic alibi**: his hospitalization keeps him out of the rest of the town's sight for almost the whole game, and his bed's background never changes.
> - **The echo with Flowery**: at the start of Ch.5, Flowery tells Kris "You've grown so much, haven't you?" — nearly identical to what Rudy tells him in the Weird Route of Ch.4 ("you've really grown up a lot, Kris").

> [!question]- The black deer in the Ch.3 retro minigame
> Inside the "Original Game" (the Zelda-style retro minigame from the Sword Route, in its third and final round, right before the fight against the Shadow Mantle), there's a black, amorphous enemy that copies the player's own movements (HERO_SWORD). Digging through the game's files reveals that this enemy has an unused backup sprite — hidden behind an internal variable literally named `toriel`, enabled by default — that turns out to be a **black deer**. The other enemy in that same round, "White Cloak," hides a *brown* deer as its backup sprite in the same way. Since the Holidays are the only confirmed deer family in the cast, the near-unanimous reading is that the black deer represents Dess — and the brown one, more disputed, Noelle — deliberately hidden behind that variable named after Toriel. It's one of the most cited file-based clues in favor of "Dess is the Knight," carrying more weight than most since it's verifiable data in the code itself, not just a symbolic reading.
>
> ![[black_deer.png]]
> *The unused backup sprite, hidden behind the `toriel` variable — a black deer.*

> [!question]- An amalgam of Kris and Noelle?
> A minority but widely discussed reading in 2026 (popularized by a YouTube video from the channel Jorge Dogue) proposes that the Knight isn't one person in disguise, but a fusion of **[[Kris]] and [[Noelle]]**, drawing on several real mechanical and design clues:
> - **Antlers + sword**: the helmet's antlers (a Holiday family trait, also present in the Dess theory) and the sword-type weapon (associated with Kris) would coexist in the same figure because, literally, they'd belong to two different people.
> - **Physical strength outside the Dark World**: the Knight is able to carry Undyne and lift Asgore with no apparent effort in the middle of the Light World — something no other Darkner can do. That would fit an entity that isn't purely Lightner or Darkner, but both at once.
> - **Parallel with Mad Mew Mew**: Ch.5 itself features an enemy that's literally a fusion of two things — Mad Dummy and Mew Mew — which only stays merged while their desires are aligned, and whose explicit goal is freedom. The fandom reads it as a deliberate thematic mirror of what would happen to the Knight.
> - **Only two names have ever been proposed *within* the fiction as a possible Knight**: Queen tells Noelle in the Ch.3 Library that she could become a new Knight; Tenna directly suspects Kris of having opened the Ch.3 3rd Dark Fountain — and we do see him do it with our own eyes. No other character has ever been named as a candidate within the game itself.
> - **In the fight against the Knight, Kris's only available action is "hold your breath"** — as if underwater — and the game prevents attacking him even when there are clear openings to do so.
>
> 🎲 The Ch.2 Weird Route (Snowgrave) ends with Kris and Noelle sinking together into the town Lake, accompanied — per the video — by a modified version of the music that plays when fighting Endogeny, one of the True Lab amalgams in Undertale. It's a striking detail, but for now it rests on the ear of whoever first pointed it out; it's worth checking directly against the game's audio files before taking it as given.
>
> 🎲 That same scene introduces an impassable white strip at the far side of the Lake — visually similar to Undertale's Underground barrier, which Alphys explains requires at least one human soul and one monster soul to cross. It's an eye-catching hypothesis, but built on the color of a map boundary; very speculative on its own. [[Lake]]
>
> 🎲 The video stretches the idea all the way to Undertale: if Frisk were "one of the many forms" of this fusion, the Delta Rune Prophecy and Undertale's Angel prophecy could be describing the same event from two different angles. This is the most speculative part of all — it rests on reading "Frisk" as a partial anagram of "Kris" — but it connects to an idea already present in this vault: that the Prophecy's Angel could be Noelle herself (see [[Ángel|Angel]]).
>
> *Contrast note: part of this theory speculates that [[Dess]] drowned trying to cross the Lake, which would explain Noelle's obsession with reaching the other side. This vault documents a much better-founded clue about her fate — the unused Wingdings text that explicitly describes her as alive, trapped, not drowned (see [[Dess]]) — so this specific part directly conflicts with the strongest clue already available and should be treated as unconfirmed speculation, not as a verified fact.*
>
> This reading doesn't replace the Dess, Asriel, or Rudy theories above — in fact it's compatible with the idea, already present in this vault, that Kris is "the Knight himself" in some sense (see [[Kris]]); here he simply wouldn't be acting alone. **A speculative fan theory, popularized in 2026 — it combines some real mechanical clues (the attacks that avoid Kris in combat, the Knight's strength outside the Dark World, the only two names the game itself ever links to the Knight) with unverified audio and map-color readings, and with at least one point that contradicts a stronger clue already documented in this vault.**
