> [!info]+ Quick facts
> 🌼 Leader of the 7 Colored Flowers; main antagonist and final boss of Chapter 5.
> 💐 In the Light World he is the golden flower from Asgore and Toriel's wedding bouquet, kept in the attic of the flower shop.
> 👑 His only goal is to keep Asgore happy inside the Dark World, whatever it takes.
> 🗄️ He hides Ralsei in a closet the moment they arrive, so Asgore won't see him.
> ⚔️ He is the only final boss in Deltarune who must be defeated non-violently instead of by force.
> 💚 He is Ralsei's declared archrival — the community has turned their rivalry into a meme.
> 🌱 In his broken pot he keeps the shelter code and a package of Seeds with danger labels — Kris tries to destroy the code, but Susie already knew because Flowery had confided it to her beforehand, anticipating exactly that.
> 🪴 Confirmed by Flowery himself in Chapter 5: "as plants, we are neither Darkners nor Lightners; we are living beings who gain consciousness in the Dark World, capable of feeling things others cannot" [TODO: verify exact EN quote] — the textual reason this note classifies him as a "plant" rather than a Darkner.

> [!tip]- Personality profile
> Flamboyant, exaggeratedly polite in public, with an ego that doesn't match what is supposedly "just a flower." He uses Asgore's nostalgia for the past to keep him entertained and away from Kris and Susie, showing a manipulative side beneath a facade of perfect kindness. He speaks with small grammatical errors, a nod to the clumsy translations of dating sims. Despite his rivalry with Ralsei, deep down he seems to respect him — and admits, already mortally wounded, that under other circumstances they could have been good friends.

> His declared archrival. Part of Ralsei's animosity comes from jealousy — Flowery solves puzzles faster than he does, and even Floradinn grows fond of him. Despite their mutual hatred, both show a certain underlying respect: Ralsei tries to save him with magic even knowing it won't help, and Flowery admits, already wounded, that under other circumstances they could have been friends. They also share something almost no one else knows: both are aware that it's Kris who opens the Dark Fountains, though each reacts in opposite ways — Ralsei covers for him out of loyalty, Flowery points it out openly out of distrust. ## Theories

> [!question]- Determination versus his six "sisters"
> Of the 7 Colored Flowers, the other six directly mirror one of the six human SOULs from Undertale (Patience, Perseverance, Bravery, Kindness, Justice, and Integrity); Flowery, as their leader, gets the seventh: Determination — the same virtue that in Undertale defines Flowey precisely because he has no SOUL of his own. It's a design choice that reinforces his corrupt nature compared to his sisters: while they are faithful to positively-connoted virtues, he gets the only one capable of turning dangerous without a SOUL to temper it. See [[7 Flores de Colores]] for the full breakdown of all seven.
>
> ![[Flowery_screenshot_end_the_dream.png]]
> *Flowery and his six sisters, all together facing the group in the final battle at Top of Castle — Determination at the front of the rest of the virtues.*

> [!question]- Parallel with Flowey (Undertale)
> ![[Flowery_face_eyes_open.png]]
> *His talksprite with open eyes, a dark gold against a white sclera — visible only after defeating him. Flowery is neither Lightner nor Darkner: he's a living being, a sentient flower, and this eye form is exclusive to him within the game.*
>
> The very name and concept (a sentient flower tied to Asgore) invites comparisons to Undertale's Flowey, though narratively there's no confirmed connection between the two — they're distinct characters in separate universes within the same franchise.

> [!question]- The Seeds and the shelter code
> At the end of Chapter 5, after sealing the Flower Kingdom's second Fountain, Kris finds two objects buried in the soil of Flowery's broken pot: a package of **Seeds** covered in warning labels, and the paper with the shelter code. He tries to destroy the paper before anyone sees it, but Susie arrives just in time with tape and takes it out of his hands.
> 
> ![[flowery_shelter_code_scene.webp]]
> *The exact moment Susie snatches the code paper from Kris, before he finishes tearing it up.*
> 
> The Seeds themselves cannot be dropped or thrown away once picked up — the game warns that "they stick to everything and can't be disposed of" [TODO: verify exact EN quote] — a direct nod to Undertale's Entry Number 10, which describes how the golden flowers' seeds also "stick to you" and "won't let go." The package itself comes covered in several danger warnings, unusual for a simple flower seed. The parallel the fandom repeats most is with the golden flowers Chara used to try to take their own life in Undertale — if that reading is correct, Kris would end the chapter with the literal ability to do (or do to someone else) the same harm Chara did. **No official source confirms that the Seeds are poisonous or what their real purpose is — this is a fan reading based on the visual and thematic parallel with Undertale, not a fact confirmed by the game.**

> [!question]+ Why does Flowery play with his own stats, or mock them?
> When he briefly joins the group, his stats are outright absurd: LV 99, 999 HP, and 99 in Attack, Defense, and Magic — far above any other character in the game. After the Floradinn encounter, Flowery goes so far as to brag about having "maximum stats" [TODO: verify exact EN quote], to which Ralsei responds, disbelieving, "Y-you can't just give yourself 999 in everything!!" [TODO: verify exact EN quote] — making clear that Flowery assigned himself those numbers, without the game granting them "legitimately" through the normal path (leveling up, equipment, etc.).
>
> ![[Flowery_screenshot_POWER_menu.png]]
> *Flowery's stat screen: LV99, 99 across the board, and even a "Guts: 🍩🍩🍩🍩🍩" that doesn't match any other stat in the game.*
>
> The parallel with Undertale's Flowey is direct: just as Flowey could manipulate save files and treat the game's own system as something within his reach to control from outside the normal rules, Flowery does the same but with numerical stats instead of the SAVE itself — he doesn't take them seriously as a system that limits him, but as a toy he can fill in as he pleases. It's fundamentally the same kind of jab at the game's own "fourth wall," just applied to stats instead of saving. **Confirmed in the game itself that he assigned the numbers himself; the reading that this is a deliberate nod to Flowey's behavior is interpretation, not a direct statement from Toby Fox.**

> [!question]- What does the code "0915199X" mean?
> The shelter code Flowery kept is "0915199X". The most widespread and best-supported reading is that "0915" is a direct nod to Undertale's release date (September 15). The final "X" deliberately leaves the year unconfirmed, within the 1990s — which has led to another, entirely unconfirmed, parallel theory that this full date might actually correspond to Asgore and Toriel's own wedding, the one the bouquet with the 7 Colored Flowers came from. Both readings can coexist without necessarily contradicting each other: the game may be winking at the player with Undertale's real release date while, within the fiction itself, that same date corresponds to a different event in Asgore's life. **The reference to Undertale (09/15) is practically confirmed by the design itself; the reading that it's the wedding date is fan theory, not confirmed.**

> [!question]- The other 6 Colored Flowers
> Alongside Flowery, they make up the 7 Colored Flowers from Asgore and Toriel's wedding bouquet. Unlike him — who is the equivalent of Undertale's Flowey — the other six directly represent, one by one, the six human SOULs from Undertale (Patience, Perseverance, Integrity, Bravery, Kindness, and Justice), both in color and in the object they use as a weapon in battle. All of them gain sentience when Flower King's Dark World opens in Chapter 5 and devote their new lives to giving Asgore back, each in their own way, the years of care they received.
>
> Their individual backstories, the parallel with Undertale's SOULs, and their relationship with Berdly, Kris, and Pink are covered in their own note — see [[7 Flores de Colores]].
>
> Pink acts as another flower in the group and Orange calls her "big sister," but she isn't one of the 7 original Colored Flowers from Asgore and Toriel's bouquet — she only "officially" joins the group after being defeated and introduced by Orange to the rest. Her real identity (a Blook family ghost possessing the Mew Mew action figure) is confirmed and covered in her own note — see [[Mad Mew Mew (Pink)]].

> [!question]- Is his "we could have been friends" to Ralsei an allusion to Undertale?
> Very likely yes, and it's no coincidence. Ralsei is widely confirmed as a mirror of Asriel — his name is an anagram of Asriel's, and his design is revealed to be almost identical to Asriel's at the end of Chapter 1 — while Flowery is, according to the official wiki itself, a direct and deliberate allusion to Flowey: both are golden flowers with high sentience, aware of the game's own mechanics like saving, able to fake their stats, with an "Omega" form that uses the combined power of six other beings, and tied in the same way to their respective father figure (Asgore/Toriel). Flowery's own musical theme, "Your Dad's Best Friend," is a direct nod to "Your Best Friend," Flowey's battle theme.
>
> The key is that, in Undertale, Flowey and Asriel **are literally the same person** — a boy without a SOUL turned into a flower. Deltarune, on the other hand, splits that single idea into two distinct, opposing characters: Ralsei (the side with a heart, with a SOUL, the protagonist) and Flowery (the side without a SOUL, cynical, the antagonist). Under this reading, when Flowery tells Ralsei that under other circumstances they could have been friends, it's not just a closing line for their rivalry — it's almost a metatextual comment: in the other game of the same saga, technically they already are, because they're the same person. **A fan reading well grounded in officially confirmed design parallels, though the exact intent of the script (that the line is a deliberate nod to that duality) isn't confirmed word-for-word by Toby Fox.**
>
> ![[flowery_goodbye_scene_alusion_ralsei.jpg]]
> *"Maybe in another life, we could have... been friends..." — the moment that sums up this whole reading.*

## Stats-menu nickname
When he briefly joins the group in Chapter 5, the POWER menu gives him the title "Roommate," with the description "Your dad's his best friend" — a literal summary of how he's wormed his way into Asgore's life while living hidden in the flower shop, and the same joke that names his musical theme, "Your Dad's Best Friend" (itself a direct nod to "Your Best Friend," Flowey's battle theme in Undertale). It's his only known title — it doesn't change with the player's choices the way Kris's, Susie's, Ralsei's, or Noelle's do.
