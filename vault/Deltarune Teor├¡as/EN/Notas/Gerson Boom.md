> [!info]+ Quick facts
> 🔨 Blacksmith, historian, and best-selling author, writer of the *Lord of the Hammer* saga (inspired by the Prophecy).
> ⚰️ He died before the start of the game; his grave is in Hometown's cemetery, with the epitaph "[TODO: verify exact EN quote] RENOWNED HISTORIAN, AUTHOR, AND TEACHER."
> 👻 Revived as a Darkner (the "Old Man") thanks to the Dark Sanctuaries created by the Roaring Knight in Ch.4.
> 🎓 Susie's mentor throughout the chapter — his secret boss fight is Gerson himself as the Hammer of Justice.
> ✍️ His son Alvin didn't follow in his footsteps as a writer for fear of always living in his shadow.
> 🐢 A returning character from Undertale, where he was the main inspiration for Undyne.
> 📜 In a discarded prophecy plaque, he's directly named "THE TORTOISE," as the forger of the Sanctuary's magic axe.

> [!tip]- Personality profile
> Wise, wisecracking, and not the least bit solemn despite his reputation — his most memetic line is simply "I'm old!" He pretends to be slow, absent-minded, and senile to test the group's patience, but in reality he knows the terrain perfectly and steers their missteps toward the puzzles' real solutions. He encourages Susie and Kris to "read between the lines" instead of following the Prophecy to the letter, insisting that even his own book was just one interpretation of something earlier and that stories can be rewritten. As a boss, he adjusts his own attack to Susie's health to always give her a fair shot, and he's willing to hand over the weapon she wins even if she loses the fight.

## From blacksmith to fantasy author — the real backstory
Gerson began his life working as a blacksmith, before becoming a historian and writing history books. Later, to entertain his own children, he made the leap into fiction and published *Lord of the Hammer*, a saga that would go on to become a critical and commercial success — the back cover of the first volume, which Kris can find signed in his own home from Ch.1 onward, describes him as "[TODO: verify exact EN quote] the acclaimed historian Gerson Boom."

![[gerson_history_of_humans_and_monsters.png]]
*Image placeholder: the signed copy of "The History of Humans and Monsters" that Kris can find in his own house from Ch.1 onward — his first book, before turning to fiction.*

At some point he also became a teacher at Hometown's only school — among his students were Green Fire and Flannel Demon, who showed up late in exchange for leftover crepes from QC's cafeteria, something Gerson happily accepted. When he died, Alphys took over his position as teacher, a succession much of the fandom reads as a deliberate echo of how Alphys herself succeeded W. D. Gaster as Royal Scientist in Undertale after his disappearance — in both cases, it's implied that the weight of succeeding someone so brilliant was a considerable burden for whoever came after.

> [!question]- Another echo of Gaster?
> Beyond Alphys's succession, the very pattern of a "vanished genius whose legacy weighs on the next person" inevitably recalls the disparity between Gaster and his Followers in Undertale (see [[Gaster (W. D. Gaster)]]). The difference is that here it does close on an explicit note of hope: Gerson actively encourages Alvin (and Susie) to write their own story without fear of falling short, something Gaster never does for anyone. **Thematic parallel noted by the official wiki, not a hidden narrative clue.**

Following monster tradition, a symbolic hammer was buried in his grave when he died — but Alvin, secretly, kept the real hammer and his father's ashes, stored in a glass display case in his office at the church. That same dust-covered hammer is, literally, what allows the Dark Sanctuary to "revive" him as a Darkner in Ch.4.

*Susie discovering the display case with Gerson's hammer and ashes in Alvin's office — the key piece confirming that the Old Man is nothing more than a temporary revival.*

## *Lord of the Hammer*: the book that mirrors the Prophecy itself
During the fight against the Hammer of Justice itself, Gerson narrates, chapter by chapter, a summary of his saga — and its structure lines up, almost move for move, with that of Deltarune's own chapters. Each chapter he narrates is also accompanied by a color change in the fight's background stained glass:

**Chapter 1, "The March of the Dark King":** the heroes defeat the king and stop the dragon.

![[lord_of_hammer_cap1.png]]

**Chapter 2, "The City of Radiance":** the heroes battle in chariots to save the Queen.

![[lord_of_hammer_cap2.png]]

**Chapter 3, "The Isles of the Northern Light":** the heroes travel between islands and catch a glimpse of a lost land.

![[lord_of_hammer_cap3.png]]

**Chapter 4, "The Trials of the Sacred Hammer":** a great blacksmith gives the heroes a terrible weapon.

![[lord_of_hammer_cap4.png]]

**Chapter 5, "The Pink and Gold Field":** the vast garden burns in an inferno of jealousy.

![[lord_of_hammer_cap5.png]]

And there it stops: the saga was left unfinished. According to Gerson himself, the story grew so immense that it "swallowed the author whole," and the young man meant to continue it — with the pen already waiting on the desk — never came to pick it up. It's a detail with a double reading: within the fiction it points directly at Alvin, who didn't follow in his father's footsteps; but it also works as a metafictional echo of Deltarune's own development, released in installments, with a Chapter 5 that took a long time to arrive.

![[Lord_of_the_Hammer_library_stant.webp]]
*The first volume of "Lord of the Hammer" on the library shelf, available from Ch.1.*

> [!question]- A real parallel with J. R. R. Tolkien
> Gerson's life notably recalls that of J. R. R. Tolkien himself: both were historians and religiously trained teachers before becoming acclaimed fantasy authors, and both left their work unfinished when they died. The difference lies in the sons: while Christopher Tolkien devoted much of his life to editing and publishing his father's unfinished texts (the *Unfinished Tales*, letters, etc.) up until his own death in 2020, Alvin never got around to continuing Gerson's work — at least not until, in Ch.5, he finally decides to write his own sermon, inspired by the (not entirely rational, as he himself admits) faith his father's letter stirred in him.

## "Reading between the lines" — his philosophy on the Prophecy
Gerson is, along with Ralsei, the game's most insistent voice against following the Prophecy to the letter. When the group accidentally stumbles into a crystal tapestry that reveals a hidden passage, Gerson jokes that they were "[TODO: verify exact EN quote] following the Prophecy so closely" that he didn't see where he was going — and if asked seriously what he thinks of prophecies, he answers that its words "[TODO: verify exact EN quote] shine too bright," that they form "[TODO: verify exact EN quote] a path so blue it's all you can see," and that he'd rather everyone walk "[TODO: verify exact EN quote] between the lines," because it's "[TODO: verify exact EN quote] darker there" and there's more room to move. It's the same philosophy he applies to his own literary legacy: *Lord of the Hammer* was, as he himself admits, only "[TODO: verify exact EN quote] the interpretation of something that came before" — and stories, he insists, "[TODO: verify exact EN quote] can be retold, can be changed."

> [!example]- The ending Susie chooses — "let everything just go on forever"
> Near the end of the fight, Gerson asks Susie how she wants "all this" to end. Susie answers that she doesn't want any ending: she'd rather things simply "keep going forever." Gerson, delighted with the answer, tells her to hold onto that idea of eternity, and that she carries "a white feather" — hope — capable of "overwriting the darkness." It's one of the few moments in the game where a character explicitly proposes an alternative to the Prophecy's own ending, instead of merely fearing it or postponing it.

## Etymology and development

> [!question]- The real origin of the name "Gerson Boom"
> Confirmed by Toby Fox himself in a 2018 Japanese interview: "Gerson" is the distorted pronunciation of the name of a Peruvian Super Smash Bros. 64 player, and specifically references a losers' round of the Tacna 2014 tournament, in which players Gerson and SuPeRbOoMfAn ("Boom," hence the surname) played such an extremely defensive style against each other that the match dragged on past 52 minutes, until Gerson was forced to attack and lost the set. Gerson's son in the game, Alvin, is named for the same reason: Alvin was the player who won that same tournament.

- During development, Toby Fox originally wanted the Dark Sanctuary to be a non-linear, Zelda-style dungeon, but scrapped the idea once he found it made Gerson's scenes hard to implement well — the final, linear structure was adopted specifically so as not to weaken his story.
- According to Temmie Chang, she animated the Old Man's idle pose based on Toby Fox himself acting it out in person using a broom as a cane.
- The line "I'm old!" from his ACT comes, according to Toby Fox himself (Undertale's 10th anniversary stream), from a high school project of his long predating Undertale.
- Jean Canellas collaborated directly with Toby Fox and AlexMdle on the full assembly of the Hammer of Justice fight, describing the process as something that "took a long time."

## Dragon Blazers — the in-universe video game based on his book
The metafictional chain doesn't stop at *Lord of the Hammer*: that same saga was adapted, within Deltarune's own world, into a series of in-universe video games called **Dragon Blazers** (at least three numbered entries). In other words, there are three nested layers: the real Prophecy → Gerson's book, which interprets it → the video game that adapts the book — and all of them, in turn, mirror Deltarune's own structure from outside the fiction.

![[dragon_blazers_cover_mockup.png]]
*Image placeholder: mockup or screenshot of Dragon Blazers, as mentioned in the game — not yet saved in the vault.*

> [!info]+ Quick facts about Dragon Blazers
> 🎮 A turn-based RPG series with at least three entries (I, II, and III), very popular among Hometown's kids.
> 🐉 One of its characters is simply "the Dragon" — the same role Gerson assigns Susie at the end of their fight.
> 💚 As Noelle explains, its battles can also be won with kindness and mercy, just like in Deltarune itself.
> ❄️ It has an Ice Palace area with a cross-shaped maze: without a guide character, the player can get stuck forever — an actual glitch the developers themselves excused in the manual as "villain magic."
> 🐭 Its block-pushing puzzles are called "Annoying Mouse," which Noelle directly compares to Deltarune's own puzzles.
> 🤮 There's a series-themed motion-sickness bag, owned by Berdly.

### Its own "Weird Route"
The second Dragon Blazers, as Noelle and Susie recount from their childhood, hid an Evil Route their parents didn't know about — Noelle played it in secret so it wouldn't be taken away from her, and Susie ended up completing it by accident, simply by not recognizing the game's pacifist options. It's an almost exact parallel to Deltarune's own Weird Route, to the point that both heroines discover, as adults, that they share that same childhood anecdote.

### The chapter-by-chapter parallels, confirmed
Beyond the summary Gerson narrates during the Hammer of Justice fight (see above), the very structure of *Dragon Blazers*/*Lord of the Hammer* mirrors Deltarune's actual chapters in a fairly literal way:
- **Ch.1, "The March of the Dark King":** the group defeats King and stops Susie from fighting, becoming her friend.
- **Ch.2, "The City of Radiance":** the group makes its way through Cyber City, faces Berdly on roller coasters, and recruits Queen.
- **Ch.3, "The Isles of the Northern Light":** the group explores the Island Board in The Legend of Tenna and catches a glimpse of the Ice Palace — the very text "[TODO: verify exact EN quote] GO TO THE NORTHERN LIGHT" that guides players to that area inside the Original Game (see [[Roaring Knight]]) is, literally, the playable version of this reference.

### Its name and other real-world nods
The title "Dragon Blazers" may be a nod to the real 1992 video game *Soul Blazer* — in fact, in Deltarune's Japanese localization, the game is literally rendered as "Dragon Blader," mirroring *Soul Blazer*'s Japanese title ("Soul Blader"). Berdly, in that same Japanese version, shortens it to "Dorabure," a clear nod to how Japanese audiences abbreviate *Dragon Quest* as "Dorakue." And in a twist very typical of Toby Fox, Dragon Blazers' actual real-world official website went on to host a link maze that replicated, almost exactly, the Spamton Sweepstakes link maze (see [[Noelle]]) — fiction and real-world merchandising feeding into each other once again.

## Trivia
- His battle ACT is simply called "Old Man," and its description is "I'm old!" — one of the most memed lines from Ch.4.
- He's a character returning directly from Undertale, where he was a historian, blacksmith, and the main inspiration for Undyne becoming a warrior.
- The twist that the "Old Man" is a revived Gerson is telegraphed fairly early on: his son Alvin already gets prominence at the start of the chapter, and there are optional hints in earlier chapters about monster funeral rituals involving the ashes of beloved objects.
- A discarded prophecy tapestry, never shown in the final game, directly names him "THE TORTOISE" and reads "[TODO: verify exact EN quote] AXE FORGED BY THE GREAT HAMMER OF THE TORTOISE" — the axe's drawing itself does appear, on its own, among the floating tapestries at the start of the 3rd Sanctuary, though without the full text.
- His battle name, "Hammer of Justice," might also reference the real song "Japan Break Industries" ("Hammer of justice, high up in the sky"), which Toby Fox himself shared on his Bluesky account in 2024.
- Several of his attacks directly reference Super Mario: his shell-shaped projectiles bounce back just like a Koopa Troopa shell, and two of his attacks (throwing hammers in an arc) recall the Hammer Bros.
