*Table note: contrary to how it might look at a glance, they do have a confirmed Real World identity — they are, literally, dice.*

> [!info]+ Quick facts
> 🎲 Diamond-shaped Darkner based on a die; common enemy of the TV World (Ch.3). His name comes from "pip," the English term for the dots on a die.
> 👀 White hexagonal head with a pair of eyes with red pupils — the right eye usually appears closed.
> 🎰 Loves gambling and money: can be persuaded to spare you instantly if bribed in battle.
> 💰 Several Pippins scam their customers out of POINTs or Dark Dollars, and steal prizes from Tenna's Bonus Zone and coins from chests — which earns them the dislike of both the Zappers and Tenna himself.
> 🎭 Three specific Pippins disguise themselves as the fake "Mikes" of Ch.3-4: Small Mike/Battat (a green Pippins, the only one of that color in the whole game), while Cat Mike/Pluey and Cowboy Hat Mike/Jongler are actually a Shadowguy and a Zapper disguised as Pippins, not real Pippins.
> 🛒 One of them sells a faked S rank for 1500 POINTs in the red-carpet hallway, letting you skip the real rank requirement to access the corresponding changing room.
> 🕹️ His name isn't mentioned in-game until Ch.3, though he makes cameos from Ch.1 onward and references to him already existed in the source code back then.

> [!tip]- Personality profile
> Being a generic enemy, he has no defined personality of his own beyond the individual attitude of each Pippins you interact with — the fake-rank seller is opportunistic, while Battat (the only one with real prominence) is talkative, insecure, and gets defensive as soon as the real Mike is mentioned.

## The green Pippins — Battat / Small Mike
The only green-colored Pippins in the whole game, distinctive enough to deserve his own section in this note. Under the disguise of "Small Mike" — one of the three impostors who take turns playing Mike so Tenna won't notice his absence — he's the most talkative and the one who takes the role most seriously, to the point of scolding his companions for not acting theirs out properly. He's revealed to be a plain green Pippins when unmasked by the group in Ch.4, and also stars in his own minigame, Battat, unlocked after defeating the trio. See [[Mike]] for the full detail of the three impostors and their role in the plot.

## Trivia
- Despite not being named until Ch.3, they appear as a cameo from Ch.1 onward, and related definitions already existed in the game's source code since that first installment.
- His name in Japanese, "Pipinzu," pluralizes as "Pippins" or "Pippinses" depending on the source.
