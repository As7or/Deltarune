*Table note: Sans has no confirmed Darkner form — he only ever appears as a Lightner, always in the Light World. He does have his own talksprite (see Personality Profile, below).*

> [!info]+ Quick facts
> 🦴 Character returning from Undertale; he settles in Hometown alongside his younger brother Papyrus right before the game begins.
> 🏪 Runs the grocery store that bears his name, calling himself its own "janitor" despite there being no one else working there.
> 👻 His arrival in town is described as abrupt and unnatural — neither Kris nor [[Susie]] remember when or how he arrived, and both comment on how out of place his house looks.
> 🍦 During the Ch.5 Festival he acts as the "ice cream Sans," and also runs the Ferris Wheel and the Teacup Ride.
> 🍅 He keeps a bottle of his own ketchup in the fridge — the same gag from Undertale.
> 💃 At the end of Ch.5 he's seen dancing with a tipsy Toriel at the Dreemurr house, confirming a close friendship between the two.

> [!tip]- Personality profile
> Just like in Undertale: laid-back, joking, and delighted to mess with whoever's nearby — he gives Kris a fake phone number that redirects to a line for "idiot babies" [TODO: verify exact EN quote] — but with genuine, protective affection toward his little brother. His manner of speech, in lowercase and using the Comic Sans font, remains unchanged from the original game.
>

## Relationships

> [!quote]- Papyrus
> His younger brother, with whom he settles in Hometown right before the game begins — both arrive as part of the same monster exodus that rebuilds much of Undertale's cast in the new town. Sans visibly worries about Papyrus's well-being, keeping the same protective dynamic they already had in the original game. [[Papyrus]]

> [!quote]- Toriel
> He becomes her friend the night before the game begins; Ch.5 confirms that closeness by showing them dancing together at the end of the Festival. His social awkwardness also shows with the rest of the family: when Asgore asks him what flower would remind him of Toriel from the day they met, all Sans can manage is that he "should talk to his plants about that." [TODO: verify exact EN quote] In Ch.4, he seems fairly unbothered by Susie's visible discomfort when she and Kris come home to find Toriel dancing with him. [[Toriel]]

## Theories

> [!question]+ Does the Ferris wheel stop halfway to hide the Lake?
> Confirmed by the game itself: the narration explicitly states "*the Ferris wheel only went up halfway*," [TODO: verify exact EN quote] and whoever rode it — Susie or Noelle, depending on who you send up — comments afterward, "*I wish you could've seen the lake... it's beautiful*," [TODO: verify exact EN quote] making it clear that the Lake IS visible from the top, but the Ferris wheel never gets there. Sans is the one operating the ride throughout the Festival.
>
> There's no line from Sans explaining why it stops exactly halfway — it could simply be a malfunction or a safety decision with no deeper meaning — but the coincidence with the fact that the Lake is, by far, the most mystery-laden setting in all of Ch.5 (the end of "Insert Chapter 7, Side B" happens precisely there) makes it tempting to read as deliberate. That said, this is a theory built on an absence of explanation, not on direct confirmation that Sans specifically is hiding something.
>
> ![[sans_wheel_noelle_dialogue_lake.png]]
> *"I wish you could've seen the lake... it's beautiful." [TODO: verify exact EN quote] — Sans's Ferris wheel, and confirmation that the Lake can indeed be seen from the top.*

> [!question]+ Did he arrive in Hometown from another dimension?
> The strongest theory about Sans: his "abrupt and unnatural" arrival in Hometown — which neither Kris nor Susie remember witnessing — would fit with the implication that Sans used his old lab (from Undertale) to travel between dimensions or timelines during the war between humans and monsters, ending up in the world of Deltarune as a result. This would also explain why he seems able to use magic (or teleport) without any other monster in town ever questioning it — something already associated with his character back in Undertale's context.
>
> ![[sans_lab_machine_another_universe.png]]
> *"There's a strange machine behind the curtain... it looks broken." [TODO: verify exact EN quote] — the Undertale lab, the centerpiece of this theory.*

> [!question]+ Is the Weird Route/Sword what "produces" or unlocks Undertale?
> One of the fandom's most widespread theories (documented even in press coverage of Ch.5): taking the Weird Route/Sword all the way to the end — including the Twisted Sword, the strongest weapon in the game to date, only obtainable by NOT following Noelle to the Lake — could be exactly what's needed for the player to finally meet W.D. Gaster, the Undertale scientist never seen in person in Deltarune. A more extreme variant, also cited in theory forums and wikis, proposes that Deltarune takes place *before* Undertale but only exists *because* Sans, at some later point, fixes his time machine and travels back to prevent the war between humans and monsters — meaning Deltarune's very existence would be a later consequence of Undertale's events, not simply a chronological "before." Neither variant is confirmed by Toby Fox; both are speculative readings built on the deliberate ambiguity over which order the two games actually occur in.

> [!question]- Does he know more than he lets on about Kris?
> In Undertale, Sans is famous for being aware of the player's resets and timelines. Carrying that same sensitivity over to Deltarune feeds the theory that Sans might perceive, even partially, the Player/Angel's influence over Kris — his line "...take it easy, ok, kid?" in the Weird Route of Ch.5 reads, under this interpretation, as a conscious warning rather than a random kind remark.
>
> ![[sans_knows_something_take_it_easy.png]]
> *"...take it easy, ok, kid?" — the line that fuels this reading.*

> [!question]+ "The place where it rained": Is Hometown Undertale's "somewhere else"?
> A confirmed fact, not just a theory: "The place where it rained" — the theme that plays at the end of Ch.4, as Kris and Susie walk through Hometown after sealing the church's Fountains — is officially a version of "It's Raining Somewhere Else" from Undertale, the song that played during Sans's dinner at the MTT Hotel. The main difference between the two is that the Deltarune version removes any reference to Sans's leitmotif, keeping the rest of the melody intact.
>
> The fandom's own reading (backed by the titles of both tracks) is that the "somewhere else" in Undertale's original song could have already been referring, even back then, to the world of Deltarune itself — meaning Toby Fox had been hinting at this world's existence for years before announcing it. As an additional clue, the description of Undertale's "Rain" achievement calls the umbrella area in Waterfall "the place where it rains," [TODO: verify exact EN quote] reinforcing the wordplay between both titles. There's still no direct textual confirmation from Toby Fox of this reading — the only official thing is the musical kinship between the two tracks themselves.
>
> ![[sans_it_rained_somewhere_else.png]]
> *The actual covers of both soundtracks — the "somewhere else" that this theory connects to Hometown.*

> [!question]+ The "don't forget" drawing and the lab
> The strongest and best-documented connection between Sans and Gaster isn't one of kinship, but of a shared object: in Undertale, behind a locked door in Sans's hidden workshop (an extension of Gaster's old research into quantum physics and parallel timelines), there's a photograph of "three people smiling, badly drawn" [TODO: verify exact EN quote] with the words "don't forget" [TODO: verify exact EN quote] written beside it. In Ch.5 of Deltarune, Sans asks Susie to draw his friends on a stamp card "so he won't forget it" — and on the Weird Route, that card shows exactly three people (Susie, Kris, and Ralsei, leaving out Noelle), while outside that route the number of people can vary and reach as high as four. The fact that the game itself reproduces that object almost to the letter — same motif, same phrase, same number "three" in the version that resembles it most — suggests Sans is still deliberately preserving something inherited from that research, and that the Weird Route might be the one that comes closest to connecting Deltarune to the events of Undertale. There's no textual confirmation of why exactly three people and not four, which keeps the theory alive.
>
> ![[sans_lab_draw_of_three_people.png]]
> *"It's a poorly-made drawing of three people smiling." [TODO: verify exact EN quote] — the original Undertale object, the piece this theory directly compares to the three-person stamp card Susie draws on the Weird Route.*
>
> An even more widespread reading goes a step further: that Sans didn't just inherit the workshop, but was some kind of **Gaster's apprentice** before he disappeared — the so-called "wrong number song" (a musical clue tied to a phone number starting with "G"), the cross-references between Sans's Gaster Blasters and Gaster himself, and the line "so, what do you two think?" [TODO: verify exact EN quote] from Entry 17 (addressed to two people, never fully identified) are the most commonly cited pieces of evidence. Still, this remains an Undertale theory carried over to Deltarune by association, not something Deltarune itself confirms directly.

> [!question]- Why doesn't Papyrus appear?
> A separate but related mystery: Sans mentions his little brother starting in Ch.1, but Papyrus has never actually appeared in person in any chapter so far — he's only heard "from afar" behind his house's door. If the workshop-and-lab theory holds any truth, it's worth asking whether Papyrus's absence is somehow related to that same inherited research. See [[Papyrus]] for the full development of this mystery.

> [!question]- 🎲 The Goner doors and Sans's shortcut
> One community reading connects three things: the machine Gaster falls into in Undertale ("they say he ended up scattered across time and space"), [TODO: verify exact EN quote] the teleporting ("Goner") doors that appear inside Deltarune's Dark Worlds, and the gray door in Undertale's "Fun 66" room, which works exactly like a Goner door. The hypothesis is that Gaster's machine would essentially be just another Goner door — and that Sans's shortcuts (his ability to appear and disappear without explanation, both in Undertale and Deltarune) might depend on that same mechanism, rather than being magic, plain and simple. **A fan theory connecting several real design clues; no direct confirmation that all three things are, in fact, the same technology.**
>
> ![[sans_door_theory.webp]]
> *The community's comparison: Sans's door, a Goner door from a Dark World, and the gray door from Fun 66 — with nearly identical designs.*

> [!question]- 🎲 The bread and Mother's breadcrumb glitch
> Sans sells an item simply called "Bread" in his Hometown shop. The reference isn't a coincidence: Undertale and Deltarune are full of nods to the *Mother/EarthBound* series (from which Toby Fox draws much of his style), and in the original *Mother* itself there's a real, documented glitch known as the "Bread Crumbs Glitch" — using (or not using) bread crumbs in a very specific way lets players reach areas of the map outside the game's normal boundaries. The fandom's reading is that Sans's "Bread" could be a deliberate nod to that glitch, and not just a merchandising joke with no further weight. **A verifiable thematic and design connection with the Mother series; no confirmation that it has any hidden function within Deltarune.**
