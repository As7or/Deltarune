*Important table note: contrary to what this note used to say, Asriel **does not return** in Ch.4 or in any chapter to date — he stays away, at university, throughout the five published chapters. Everything we know about him comes from objects, photos, letters, and what others say about him.*

> [!info]+ Quick facts
> 🐐 Son of Toriel and Asgore, adoptive brother of Kris; the same Asriel Dreemurr from Undertale.
> 🎓 He's away at university and **has not appeared in person in any chapter up to and including Ch.5** — he is only mentioned, his belongings are seen, or he's heard in the background of a silhouetted flashback.
> 💌 He wrote an unsent love letter to Dess ("Dearest December"), and another one, sent, to Catty after taking her to a school dance — he seems to have been flirting with several people at once.
> 🏆 His trophy shelf in the room he shares with Kris is packed full; Kris's is completely empty — one of the most-discussed visual contrasts in the game.
> 🚪 Kris closes their eyes while snooping in Asriel's room at Queen's Mansion — the narration literally says "you didn't see anything." It's the only door in the game that Kris themself blocks so directly.
> 🌼 His time as Flowey in Undertale is never directly addressed in Deltarune — the game treats it with complete normalcy.

> [!tip]- Personality profile
> Based on everything said about him, he's open, affectionate, and very popular in Hometown — almost every one of Kris's neighbors only has good things to say about him. He has lots of trophies, likes music (including religious ska), and writes letters in a curiously formal, cheesy style. Despite his differences with Kris, he's described as someone who cares deeply about his little brother.

## Where he is, and why we never see him
As of now, Asriel is a character built entirely out of absence: the game has gone five chapters without showing him even once. Toriel mentions him excitedly in Ch.1 because he's planning to visit "next week"; Pizzapants is saving up to attend his same university; Asgore dreams of taking the whole family back to their usual diner. In Ch.2, a door with his name on it even appears in Queen's Mansion —made, like the others, from each character's browsing history— but Kris closes their eyes before anything inside can be seen.

![[asriel_room.png]]
*The room he shares with Kris: his shelves packed with trophies against Kris's, completely empty.*

In Ch.4, [[Susie]] discovers that same contrast while looking at the room's shelves: Asriel's full of trophies, Kris's entirely empty — and she takes a glow-in-the-dark star from Asriel's shelf to make Kris a trophy of his own. It's a detail that fits a much broader fan reading: Asriel's room is full of life, color, and achievements; Kris's is plain, gray, and has nothing of its own — the same washed-out palette shared, for example, by the Gaster Followers or any "copy" with no real identity within the Undertale/Deltarune universe. Under that reading, Kris's room wouldn't be empty by chance, but as another visual reflection of the Vessel theory: literally, little "of their own" to show.

## Is Kris hiding something from the Player?
This is, by far, the most direct and least-cited clue of all: when Kris snoops in Asriel's room at Queen's Mansion, the narration explicitly says "*[TODO: verify exact EN quote] you opened the door with your eyes closed. You didn't see anything.*" — Kris themself, not the player, decides not to let that room be seen. It's the only time in the whole game that such a simple action (opening a door and looking) gets blocked in a way this deliberate and this explicitly narrated as a choice made by Kris. See [[Jugador|Player]] for the full development of this tension.

And there's a second piece that fits almost perfectly: when trying to **Check** the Roaring Knight during battle, the game responds with the exact same structure — first "*[TODO: verify exact EN quote] (You get the feeling you wouldn't learn anything.)*", and if you press further, "*[TODO: verify exact EN quote] But Kris couldn't learn anything.*" It's the same verb ("learn"/"see"), the same total negation, and the same implicit subject: it's specifically Kris who cannot or will not let something be known — not a generic Check-system failure, which normally does give some description, however minimal.

Taken together with other scenes where Kris hides specific information from the player —erasing the call log after warning the mysterious person that Susie found Dess's note, for example— this strongly reinforces the reading that Kris actively protects certain things from the player's control/gaze, and that both his brother and the Knight himself seem to be on that list. It's still not a direct narrative confirmation that both cases are connected to each other (it could simply be the same design device reused without any further implication), but the textual coincidence is among the strongest in the whole game for supporting the idea that Kris knows more than the player is ever allowed to see.

## The first kiss and the favorite-character change (confirmed fact + fan reading)
Confirmed by the game: as a kid, [[Berdly|Bratty]] convinced Asriel to give her his first kiss while playing truth or dare, and when Toriel found out she took the whole family to church almost daily for a week. Separately, it's also confirmed that Tenna's Ch.3 board reveals that Asriel, embarrassed, changed his favorite Super Smashing Fighters character from the "Green Lizard" (a Yoshi parody) to the "Sword Mage" (a parody of Robin, from Fire Emblem).

> [!question]- Do his favorite characters reflect the order of his romantic interests?
> A reading from an analysis video (not confirmed by the game): the order of those two favorite characters would match the real order of his interests — first Bratty (his first kiss, tied to the Green Lizard), then someone represented by the Sword Mage, a character who attacks with swords, the Knight's signature weapon. It's a symbolic reading, not a direct textual clue; the game itself only confirms Asriel's embarrassment over the change, not the reason for it.

## Theories

> [!question]+ Is Asriel the Roaring Knight?
> A weak theory, increasingly less supported: since Asriel still hasn't appeared in person in any chapter, technically nothing rules him out entirely — but most of the Knight's clues (the Holiday-exclusive antlers, the special restraint he shows only with Kris) point far more toward Dess than toward him.
>
> ![[asriel_roaringknight.webp]]
> ![[asriel_roaringknight2.webp]]
> *Fan comparisons between Asriel and the Roaring Knight — among the Knight's identity theories, this is currently one of the least supported.*

> [!question]- Was he present the day Dess disappeared?
> Unconfirmed. The game doesn't explicitly link Asriel to the specific incident that made Dess disappear, nor to the creation of the first Dark World — we only know that, in the family's general TV-watching flashback, he vanishes from the scene after her, not before or during. Whether he was there that specific day, or whether his absence from Hometown already predated it, remains a completely open question.

> [!question]+ Is Ralsei actually Asriel?
> The oldest and most-debated identity theory in the whole game. The evidence in favor:
> - **The physical resemblance**: according to Susie and Noelle, Ralsei without his hat is nearly a carbon copy of Asriel. Noelle says it directly to Susie in Ch.4 ("[TODO: verify exact EN quote] he looked a little like Asriel... but not"), and Susie, on seeing an actual photo of Asriel that same chapter, at first thinks it's a Photoshop of Ralsei.
> - **Kris steps in unprompted**: in Ch.3, if the player hints they've seen Ralsei's face before, Kris laughs and, unasked, assures them they don't think the two look that much alike — almost as if trying to convince themself. In Ch.4, when Susie confuses Asriel's photo with Ralsei, Kris again interrupts to point out "all the differences" between the two, again on their own initiative.
> - **The name**: "Ralsei" is an anagram of "Asriel," and also comes from "Ralse," the protagonist of a game by Toby Fox's older brother.
>
> The evidence against:
> - **Tenna says nothing**: despite having seen both Asriel (as a regular viewer) and Ralsei (throughout Ch.3), Tenna never comments on any resemblance between the two — odd if they really were physically identical.
> - **Asgore, upon meeting Ralsei in Ch.5**, does note the resemblance to Asriel at first, but on closer look directly doubts whether Ralsei is even a monster — a reaction that doesn't fit well with the idea that he's literally looking at his own son.
> - **Asriel still hasn't appeared in person** in any chapter to date, which prevents a direct comparison and leaves the theory at a standstill: neither side can be fully confirmed or ruled out with what's available right now.
>
> Overall, it's a strong theory given the amount of direct textual clues (especially Kris's reactions, which seem deliberately written to draw attention to the subject), but without any definitive confirmation.

## Trivia
- He's the same character as in Undertale, confirming that both games share a universe and cast rather than being separate reboots.
- His absence throughout the whole game (studying away) is one of the narrative reasons Kris starts out with practically no friends.
- The religious ska CD hidden under his bed is a real contribution from Reid Young (Fangamer co-founder), who introduced Toby Fox to Five Iron Frenzy's discography.
- His name is one of the few words that triggers a unique response from the Voice in the Ch.1 intro.
- Nicknames others give him: "Azzy" (Pizzapants, Catty, Bratty), "Doug/Douglas" (Catty's father, who isn't too fond of him), "the brother who smells like pizza" (Susie, jokingly).
