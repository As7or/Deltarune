*Table note: Everyman has no talksprite or dialogue of his own in either game — he's purely a recurring visual nod.*

> [!info]+ Quick facts
> 🥚 A white, cartoonish humanoid figure, with a long beak-shaped protrusion and a hollow eye in the center of its head.
> 🔁 An easter-egg character inherited directly from Undertale, where he was the attack method of the amalgamate Reaper Bird.
> 🏷️ His internal name in both games' files is **"strangeman,"** not "Everyman" — the public name comes from Undertale's official Art Book.
> 🎪 In Deltarune he reappears with practically no design changes, almost always with no narrative weight.
> 🖼️ He appears in Jevil's carousel attack, in Poppup's pop-up window attack, in Queen's "Social Media" attack, and in a piece of graffiti in Hometown's alley next to ICE-E.
> 💰 He also appears non-canonically in fake reviews and donations from the Spamton Sweepstakes (a real charity event).

> [!tip]- Personality profile
> He has no personality of his own — he's a literal "everyman" archetype, an ordinary, benign character used as a contrast against flashier protagonists. His function is to be a recognizable nod for anyone who knows the reference, not a piece of the plot.

## Appearances

> [!example]+ In Undertale — Reaper Bird
> He serves as the main attack method of the amalgamate Reaper Bird, in the True Lab: curiously, Everyman doesn't deal direct damage to the protagonist on his first hit — it's Reaper Bird itself that attacks him, covering him in butterflies (named "obj_strangeman_butterfly" in the game's files) and making him writhe in pain, ending up headless by the end of the animation. Only in later turns, when Reaper Bird uses him to throw copies of its own head or (in versions before v1.001) to display the enemy's internal stats overlaid on screen, does Everyman become the only point through which the player can take real damage in the whole fight.
>
> Neither Reaper Bird nor Everyman appear in the epilogue alongside the rest of the amalgamates, unlike Lemon Bread, Endogeny, or Snowdrake's Mother.

> [!example]+ In Deltarune
> He reappears at several points with no apparent narrative connection between them:
> - In the second version of Jevil's carousel attack (Ch.1), one of the alternate duck faces has a 1-in-50 chance of being replaced by Everyman's head.

> - In Queen's "Social Media" attack (Ch.2), his sprite also appears as part of the fake posts she throws in battle — the same sprite that would later be reused in the Spamton Sweepstakes.

## Relationships

> [!quote]- Spamton
> He shares with him the nature of a nod tied to the series' real merchandising — Everyman appears in the Spamton Sweepstakes, the charity event associated with Spamton. [[Spamton]]

> [!quote]- FRIEND
> Both belong to the "Easter Egg"/"Other" category on the official wiki, characters that exist more as a nod than as a piece of the main plot. [[FRIEND]]

> [!quote]- Gaster
> They share a very specific internal naming pattern: in Undertale's files, only six names end in "-man" — *strangeman* (Everyman), *mysteryman* (Gaster), *riverman* (River Person), *watchingman* (the True Lab's bed-amalgamate), *darkman* (the world sprite for the Core's enemies), and *snowman*. This coincidence, added to the fact that both are tied to the True Lab, is the basis for one of the fandom's more elaborate theories about his nature. [[Gaster (W. D. Gaster)]]

## Theories

> [!question]+ Is Everyman actually [[Berdly]]?
> One of the fandom's most cited theories, born from the fact that Berdly never appears or is mentioned in Undertale, leaving open who his "version" in that game might be. In favor: Everyman's beak resembles a bird, just like Berdly; he specifically replaces the head of a duck (a bird) in Jevil's carousel; he appears in Queen's "Social Media" attack, a character Berdly himself admires and tries to impress for much of Ch.2; and the graffiti depicting him is in Hometown, where Berdly lives. Against: none of these coincidences is narratively confirmed, and Everyman's own design predates Berdly's creation by years. **Fan theory, with several accumulated thematic coincidences; unconfirmed.**

> [!question]- Is he connected to Gaster?
> Based on the pattern of internal "-man" names (see above, in Relationships), part of the fandom argues that Everyman could be related to Gaster in some way — perhaps as a prior experiment, a leftover from his research at the True Lab, or simply an entity "adjacent" to that same circle of narrative mystery — rather than being a simple visual joke with no backstory. The theory, developed in depth by fan analyses focused on mining the game's internal files, has no support within the dialogue or playable plot of either title. **Fan theory, based on real file data but with entirely speculative interpretation; unconfirmed.**

> [!question]- Why the unused Ch.4 variable?
> The discovery of `everyman_appeared` in the code for the scene before the Titan, in Ch.4's church, fuels speculation that Everyman might have had, in some development draft, a real appearance (even a minor one) in that scene. Since it was never implemented or referenced by any other known code fragment, it's impossible to know what form it would have taken. **Confirmed file data (the variable exists); its actual purpose is pure fan speculation.**

> [!question]- Who is Everyman, really?
> His nature is never explained within the fiction — neither in Undertale nor in Deltarune. Beyond the Berdly and Gaster theories above, some broader readings interpret him simply as the "equivalent of a stick figure" for monsters: a neutral human figure with no identity of its own, meant to represent "anyone" within the fiction, just as his own name indicates. Everyman appears deliberately designed to have no clear answer.

## Trivia
- His name is confirmed directly by Undertale's official Art Book, with the description "Just a good guy who shows up on occasion" ("Solo un buen tipo que aparece de vez en cuando").
- He's one of the few characters, alongside ICE-E and the Annoying Dog, to have been seen in both the Light World and the Dark World, as well as in Undertale.
- In Undertale's official Art Book he's grouped together with Knight Knight and Whimsalot — "sword and sorcery" type Core enemies — which some fans read as an additional design clue, though it has no confirmed relation to Deltarune's Roaring Knight despite the coincidence of the English name ("Knight").
- Toby Fox confirmed his name and descriptive line ("Everyman") publicly on Twitter back in 2015, even before Undertale's release.
