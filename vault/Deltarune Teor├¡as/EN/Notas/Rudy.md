*Table note: Rudy has no confirmed Darkner form — he only appears as a Lightner, always at the hospital. He does have his own talksprite (see Personality profile, below).*

> [!info]+ Quick facts
> 🦌 Father of Noelle and Dess, husband of Carol; bedridden at Hometown Hospital.
> 📅 Visitable at the end of Ch. 1 and 2, and during the Lightworld segments of Ch. 4 and 5.
> 😄 Jovial and a joker — recalls Sans in the way he lightens the mood despite his health.
> 🎓 He was Asgore's university classmate; met Toriel through the campus social scene, and remembers her as much wilder in her youth.
> 💥 He met Carol when, the moment he saw her, he told her she was "a HOT piece of work" — she responded with a slap that knocked him out. They still ended up getting married.
> 🕯️ He keeps a faceless angel doll in his room, made by Dess and Noelle as kids, as a good-luck charm.
> ⛪ In Ch.4, after the church service, he returns to the hospital exhausted from the effort — promising to regain his strength in time for the Festival with Noelle.
> 🪜 In Ch.5, already out of the hospital, he helps set up the Festival lights up on a ladder. If, on the normal route, Noelle ignores Carol's call during her date with Susie, Rudy falls off the ladder and lies on the ground for hours before anyone finds him — the "something terrible" Carol announces over the phone.

> [!tip]- Personality profile
> Outgoing, honest, and with a sense of humor that doesn't fade even stuck in a hospital bed — he jokes about almost anything, including his own illness ("[TODO: verify exact EN quote] I'm bored as HELL in here"). Very protective of Noelle, openly supports her interest in [[Susie]], and can't stand [[Berdly]], whom he calls "Nerdly" and jokingly threatens to throw out the hospital window.

## Theories

> [!question]+ The fifth to "vanish" — a scene, not a real disappearance
> In the flashback Tenna shows before her fight (Ch.3), where the Holiday and Dreemurr families are seen gathered watching TV together, each family member vanishes one after another **from that specific scene**: first Dess, then Carol, Asgore and Noelle, and Rudy fifth and last — matching Carol's line that Rudy has "fallen down," the same euphemism used for his hospitalization. Unlike Dess, Rudy doesn't actually disappear or stop appearing for the rest of the game — he's still present in Ch.4 and Ch.5, admitted while he recovers. The order of that list (which includes Asgore, not just the Holidays) suggests a deeper connection between the two families than it first seems, but it in no way implies that Rudy is also missing.
>
> ![[The_Dreemurrs_and_Holiday_Families.webp]]
> *Tenna's flashback: the Holiday and Dreemurr families gathered in front of the TV — the scene each of them vanishes from, one by one.*

> [!question]+ Is Rudy the Roaring Knight?
> One of the community's most-discussed identity theories for the Knight — with serious supporters outside this vault too, not just here. The strongest points:
> - **Clear motivation**: if the Knight is trying to create Dark Fountains to get someone lost back, Rudy shares exactly that motivation regarding Dess — the same as Carol, but without the suspicion weighing on her because of her public role as mayor.
> - **The Holiday link**: the Knight has antlers, and so far the only consistent design trait it shares with the family is exactly that — deer antlers, the Holidays' species.
> - **Reacts warily to Susie on the Ch.4 Weird Route** (see above) — asking Kris, annoyed, why he brought her, while remaining the same as ever with Kris himself; a selective distrust toward someone who, not having been part of "before," never fully got to know him.
> - **Geographic alibi**: his hospitalization keeps him out of the rest of the town's sight for almost the whole game, and his bed's background never changes — the most static "camera" of the whole cast, matching the pattern of other Knight candidates (prolonged physical absence with no witnesses).
> - Ralsei himself explains that creating a Dark Fountain involves channeling Determination intensely — this would thematically fit an illness that worsens as the game progresses, though the text itself never explicitly connects the two.
> - **The echo with Flowery**: at the start of Ch.5, Flowery greets Kris with "Kris. You've grown so much, haven't you?" — a line almost identical to the one Rudy says to him on the Ch.4 Weird Route ("[TODO: verify exact EN quote] you really have grown up a lot, Kris"). The community connects this as part of a broader parallel between Rudy, Flowery, and the Knight (there's a YouTube video, "Parallels between Rudy, Flowery, and The Knight," dedicated entirely to this comparison), though the line by itself is a generic "you've grown so much" greeting any adult could say to Kris, so its real weight depends on whether you accept the broader parallel or not.
>
> Much weaker points, which do circulate as part of the theory but rest on wordplay or unsupported chains of assumption: that the Knight's attacks are red and "Rudy" sounds like "ruddy" (reddish), or that the name evokes Rudolph the red-nosed reindeer; that Rudy's dominant hand is never shown on screen; or that he could have learned to use a katana thanks to Carol. These are part of how the theory gets argued in forums and videos, but don't add much on their own.
>
> **A consistent and fairly debated fan theory, with genuinely suggestive motivation and design clues — but the game never confirms or denies the Knight's identity, and several of the arguments supporting it are fairly loose speculation.**

- His constant background hospitalization makes him one of the few characters whose "camera" never changes setting throughout the whole game.
