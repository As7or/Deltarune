*Table note: no confirmed sprite of his face or full body exists — his real appearance is, by design, a total mystery. The official wiki titles his page simply "Man"; this note uses "Forgotten Man" because that's the name he introduces himself with in Ch.3 ("I AM THE FORGOTTEN MAN"), which is more descriptive for a standalone note.*

> [!info]+ Quick facts
> 🌳 Always hides behind a tree with red leaves, identical to the ones found in the Field and the Forest of Ch.1 — his body and face are never seen.
> 🥚 Gives Kris an Egg once per chapter, in a different secret area each time — see [[Huevo|Egg]] for the item itself.
> 🔁 Acknowledges that Kris has forgotten him "many times" before, and that even if he isn't forgotten this time, he'll end up forgotten again — hence his own name.
> 🗣️ Only has actual dialogue (beyond narration) starting in Ch.3, in Mancountry.
> 🏝️ Mancountry, his personal "kingdom" within Ch.3, has a retro monochrome green aesthetic, very different from the rest of the game — a visual nod to the Game Boy and to the Legend of Tenna.
> 👶 In Ch.4, Kris finds him in a hospital-like room that turns out to be a childhood art therapy scene — the most cited clue from any of his appearances.

> [!tip]- Personality profile
> Friendly, curious, and somewhat melancholic. He likes to know the opinion of whoever he's talking to and asks for it often. He speaks about the creation of the world in terms that recall the real origin of Earth, and claims to have existed "since the dawn of the world." Despite his unsettling nature — always hidden, always forgotten — he's never hostile or threatening toward Kris.

## The 2024 Valentine's card — his first real appearance
Even before he had any dialogue within the game, the Forgotten Man had already introduced himself outside of it. In Toby Fox's 2024 winter newsletter (February 13), among several random Valentine's postcards from other characters, one appeared that was extremely rare, from an unknown sender: it wished a happy new year, asked [TODO: verify exact EN quote] "How's Deltarune doing?", talked about time continuing to pass while people wait, and confessed to wanting to help someone whose name had been forgotten — closing with instructions to [TODO: verify exact EN quote] "wash your face and get dressed." That same postcard was replaced shortly after by a blank version, with no text.

![[man_valentines_card.webp]]
*The 2024 Valentine's postcard, from an unknown sender — before the Forgotten Man had a single line of dialogue within the game.*

The connection to the Ch.3 Forgotten Man is practically official through textual repetition: his dialogue in Mancountry repeats that same closing line almost word for word ([TODO: verify exact EN quote] "wash your face and get dressed"), and both share the same tone of someone speaking with the knowledge that they'll be forgotten. It's by far the strongest and earliest piece of evidence (more than a year before Ch.3) that the Forgotten Man is a character planned well before his playable appearance.

> [!question]- Did Gaster write that card, or was it the Forgotten Man himself?
> When the postcard came out, much of the fandom assumed it was Gaster because of the style (all caps, repetition, talk of being forgotten). But compared line by line with Entry Number 17 and the tweets from the Undertale account, the tone and vocabulary don't quite match — similar, but not identical. The reading that has gained the most traction over time is that the Forgotten Man himself wrote the postcard, not Gaster, since his later Ch.3 dialogue quotes that same text almost verbatim. See also the "Is he Gaster?" theory below.

## Forgotten Island and the Seven Flying Aces — the biggest clue in Ch.3
Inside Mancountry, west of the entrance, there's a cliff officially called **Forgotten Island**, presented as a "World Heritage Site." Six small Rudinns live there, identifying themselves as the **Seven Flying Aces**: they confirm they're photocopied, hand-colored copies of seven ace cards Kris used as a child to cheat at card games — later lost between the couch cushions. Only six are present (one of them jokes that Kris couldn't count properly, [TODO: verify exact EN quote] "that's why he had to cheat"); the seventh appears, in a nod much later, as the receptionist Rudinn of the hidden therapy room in Ch.4.

One of them says the line that has driven much of the Vessel theorizing: [TODO: verify exact EN quote] "the copies are monochrome, but you colored each of us with love," and another asks Kris directly why he doesn't "photocopy Kris" so he can play with them. The parallel with the Vessel itself — a "monochrome" version of Kris, with no color or identity of its own, literally created by the player — is, for much of the fandom, too direct to be a coincidence, though the game never confirms it explicitly at any point.

On that same cliff there are binoculars that, when looking south, only show gray terrain that one of the Rudinns describes feeling like "evil" — the same color code (green = life/one's own identity, gray = emptiness/copy) that already appears in the contrast between Asriel's and Kris's sides of their shared room, and in the couches of the Green Room (see the theory about Kris's envy in [[Kris]]).

> [!question]- Is Forgotten Island the Vessel itself, in another form?
> Fan theory, unconfirmed: the combination of "monochrome copies colored by Kris," the name "Flying Aces" itself (photocopied, lost, forgotten), and the "evil" gray terrain seen through the binoculars points, for part of the community, to Forgotten Island as a whole functioning as a visual metaphor for the Vessel itself and for the identity-less copies that populate the Undertale/Deltarune universe (Goners, Followers of Gaster, Clam Goner). With no direct textual confirmation that it's literally the same thing.

## Relationships

> [!quote]- Kris
> He's the only character who can find him or reach the rooms that hold him — no one else in the group is present in any of these scenes, whoever happens to be accompanying Kris at the time. The Forgotten Man himself claims to have known Kris "before," and the whole relationship revolves around mutual, repeated forgetting between the two of them. [[Kris]]

> [!quote]- FRIEND

## Theories

> [!question]+ Is he Gaster?
> The most widespread reading. For: he moves freely between the Light World and the Dark World; he speaks in timeless terms about the origin of the world; he fits the description of someone [TODO: verify exact EN quote] "shattered across space and time"; and the room he appears in during Ch.1 shares its structure with Mystery Man's room in Undertale, the internal sprite linked to Gaster. Against: his way of speaking in Ch.3 is noticeably different from the Voice's, which makes part of the fandom doubt they share an identity. **Fan theory, the most popular of the three; unconfirmed.**
>
> ![[gaster.png]]
> *Mystery Man, the internal Undertale sprite linked to Gaster — his room shares its structure with the Ch.1 room where the Forgotten Man first appears, the main visual basis for this reading.*

> [!question]- Is he a repressed trauma of Kris's?
> Kris is the only character present in any scene that leads to the Forgotten Man, without exception — no one in the group ever sees him or mentions him afterward. The Ch.3 and 4 appearances revolve explicitly around Kris's childhood, and the Ch.4 scene takes place in what looks like a childhood art therapy session — in fact, the Egg/tree/"therapy" association itself was already confirmed by Toby Fox more than a month before Ch.4's release, through a secret Spamton Sweepstakes page literally titled "Therapy" (see [[Huevo|Egg]] for the detail). Under this reading, the Forgotten Man wouldn't be a "real" character within the fiction, but the projection of a traumatic memory that Kris represses and forgets over and over — hence the character's own name — possibly tied to whatever happened with Dess or the Shelter. **A minority fan theory, but with good textual support in the Ch.3-4 scenes and now also in pre-release material; unconfirmed.**
>
> 🎲 An analysis video takes this same clue a step further, following the full Spamton Sweepstakes ARG chain (Noelle's blog → Cat Petterz 2 → `deltarune.com/windows` → "lostinthewoodsforest" → "thornchildren," the same tree "therapy" page) to propose a specific answer for exactly what's being repressed: FRIEND. See [[FRIEND]] for the full development of that clue chain.
>
> ![[man_2nd_Sanctuary_window_overworld_egg_comparison.png]]
> *The egg-shaped window in the Second Sanctuary that grants access to the Ch.4 hospital-like room — the central scene this reading rests on as a repressed childhood memory of Kris's.*

> [!question]- Is he Papyrus?
> The weakest of the three readings, based mostly on real merchandising coincidences rather than clues within the game itself: there's an official Fangamer drawing showing Papyrus hidden behind a tree with red leaves almost identical to the Forgotten Man's, and part of the fandom connects the cracked egg shape that would recall Mystery Man's head (the sprite linked to Gaster) with Papyrus's fondness for [TODO: verify exact EN quote] "that oatmeal with dinosaur eggs," mentioned in passing by Flowey in a 2016 Undertale Q&A. On top of that, another official Toby Fox Q&A drawing shows Papyrus "on a date," surrounded by a joke stat panel that includes an icon labeled, with no further explanation, "EGG" — another wink from the author himself linking Papyrus to an egg, even if only as a joke. There's no clue within Deltarune itself that supports this reading beyond those coincidences external to the game. **A highly speculative fan theory, supported by merchandising and trivia external to the game; unconfirmed.**
>

## Trivia
- He's one of the few characters in the whole game whose physical appearance is never shown or described in detail, not even partially.
- The odds of finding him in the Ch.1 Forest are 1 in 50 (2%) per backtrack between two specific rooms — the same odds, coincidentally, as other optional secrets in the game.
- Missing a chapter's Egg by not completing the sequence correctly makes it impossible to get retroactively in most cases.
