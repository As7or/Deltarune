> [!info]+ Quick facts
> 🌐 Toby Fox has confirmed that Deltarune takes place in a parallel/alternate universe with respect to Undertale — it is not a direct sequel or prequel, in his own words.
> 👥 It shares much of the cast (Toriel, Asgore, Sans, Papyrus, Undyne, Alphys, Asriel, Napstablook, Gerson, Bratty, Catty...) but their stories, relationships, and life circumstances have changed — some slightly, others completely.
> 💠 It shares the SOUL system between both games, with the same color symbolism and virtues.
> 👻 The existence and disappearance of W.D. Gaster is the most-cited and least-resolved shared thread of lore between the two games.
> 🔤 Certain texts and symbols (Wingdings, "Entry Number 17", the "666" font) appear in both games with no direct narrative explanation — as if the game's own files "remembered" things the characters shouldn't remember.
> 🃏 Ch.5 provided the most direct physical proof to date: the punch card that Sans asks to be drawn on at the Festival is, by far, the same object that already existed hidden in his workshop in Undertale since 2015 — a real object crossing from one game to the other, not just a character with the same face.

## Shared characters, one by one

A rundown of the Undertale characters who reappear in Deltarune as Hometown Lightners (or other roles), with what stays the same and what has changed in each case. Most don't yet have a saved side-by-side comparison image in `Sprites/` — this is marked as an image gap in each case except where one does exist.

> [!example]+ Toriel
> **Similarities**: warm and protective personality, a tendency toward overprotectiveness, genuine affection for Kris/Asriel/Frisk depending on the game, a past as queen/authority figure.
> **Differences**: in Deltarune she is a high school teacher in Hometown instead of the guardian of the Ruins; she's still divorced from Asgore, but here both are Asriel's biological parents and Toriel also adopted Kris — a much bigger and more "normal" family than the one in Undertale.

> **Similarities**: same design, same lazy and joking personality, same taste for bad puns, same food-cart job.

He is also the only Lightner who uses magic with total freedom outside the Dark Worlds (he teleports in the middle of Ch.4, and in Ch.5 he even runs three different ice cream stands on the same street at the same time) — breaking a rule that every other Light World character follows without exception.

> [!example]+ Asriel
> **Similarities**: he is, literally, the same character — Kris's older brother, son of Toriel and Asgore.
> **Differences**: in Deltarune he's alive, has grown up, went to college, and has come back home — none of the events of Undertale (his death as Flowey, the fusion with Chara's SOUL) seem to have happened to him here. He is the most direct and least ambiguous confirmation of the entire shared cast, because he appears physically on screen starting in Ch.4 with no doubt whatsoever about his identity. **Image gap.**

> [!example]+ Napstablook
> **Similarities**: same subdued, melancholic character, same musical tastes.
> **Differences**: he appears in a much more everyday context within Hometown, without the "lonely ghost in the Ruins/Waterfall" backstory from Undertale. **Image gap.**

> [!example]+ Gerson Boom
> **Similarities**: same trade as a veteran blacksmith/craftsman, same wisdom as the village elder.
> **Differences**: in Deltarune he gains an entire new side as a fantasy writer (*Lord of the Hammer*, see [[Gerson Boom]]) that didn't exist in Undertale — in fact, it's Undertale's own Gerson who already mentioned the Prophecy and the Angel in his shop in Waterfall, long before Deltarune existed. **Image gap.**

> [!example]+ Bratty and Catty
> **Similarities**: same pair of friends, same profile of carefree teenagers.
> **Differences**: their dynamic changes at its root — in Deltarune, Catty is no longer Bratty's best friend but her rival, and it's Bratty who orchestrates the "Pizzarina" prank against Pizzapants with Blue Ears's help. **Image gap.**

> [!example]+ Everyman
> **Similarities**: same concept of a "generic NPC with no distinguishing features," already present in Undertale as background filler.
> **Differences**: in Deltarune he has much more relative prominence as his own recurring character, with his own dedicated note in the vault (see [[Everyman]]).

## Where it appears

> [!example]+ Chapter 5 — Sans at the Festival and the punch card
> Sans appears at the ice cream stand during the Festival, running (depending on the point in the chapter) up to three different food stands on the same street at the same time. On the Weird Route he gives bread to Kris before Kris heads to the Lake — an item whose description implies it leaves a trail, a direct nod to the fact that he's still collecting breadcrumbs for his secret workshop, just as he did in Undertale.
>
> The most-cited moment of the whole chapter: Sans hands a punch card to whoever is with him and asks them to draw his friends on it, "so he'll remember." The result is always a badly-drawn image of three smiling friends, with a note that says "Don't Forget" — the very title of Deltarune's main theme. That same card, with that same drawing and that same phrase, has been hidden in a locked drawer of Sans's secret workshop in Undertale since 2015 — years before Deltarune even existed as a public concept.

## Theories

> [!question]+ Is Sans literally a traveler from another reality, come from Deltarune?
> The strongest theory in the whole note, and one of the most-cited in the whole fandom. It's built on several independent pieces of evidence:
> - **The Ch.5 punch card**: by far the most direct piece of proof — a nearly identical physical object (same drawing, same phrase "Don't Forget") already existed in Sans's secret workshop in Undertale, published in 2015, years before Deltarune was even announced. It's not a design coincidence: it's the same object in two different games.
> - **His unchanged design**: of the entire shared cast, Sans is the only one whose Light World sprite has no alteration whatsoever compared to Undertale — no new clothes, no design tweaks, just a couple of added talksprite expressions.
> - **He breaks the Light World's own rules**: every other Lightner avoids using magic outside the Dark Worlds (Asgore is just a florist, Undyne only summons spears inside Fountains) — Sans teleports openly in Ch.4 and impossibly multiplies his physical presence in Ch.5.
> - **His own dialogue in Undertale**: in the final battle of the Genocide Route, Sans admits [TODO: verify exact EN quote] "I gave up trying to go back a long time ago... and going to the surface doesn't really appeal to me anymore either," never specifying what place he meant by "go back."
> - **The sun detail with Papyrus**: on Undertale's True Pacifist Route, when all the monsters come out to the surface, Papyrus doesn't recognize the sun — strange for someone who has always lived underground, unless he had actually lived somewhere with a sky before (like Deltarune's own Hometown) and had forgotten it. Sans, on the other hand, does recognize it without hesitation.
> - 🎲 **The Genocide Route blood**: in a rarely-seen Undertale battle sprite, only accessible under very specific Genocide Route circumstances, Sans appears bleeding from the mouth with a gash on his torso.
>
> ![[sans_bleeding.webp]]
> *Sans bleeding — the Genocide Route battle sprite the fandom cites as a biological anomaly.*
>
> By Undertale's own rules, monsters don't have blood — they turn to dust when they die instead of bleeding, let alone one made entirely of bone. A bleeding Sans is, therefore, an anomaly within his own universe's rules, which the fandom reads as one more crack in the idea that he doesn't quite fit that world — consistent with the theory that he actually comes from somewhere else. Interestingly, Deltarune has its own echo of this same anomaly: at the end of Ch.4, [[Susie]]'s hand bleeds visibly and unambiguously right as she tears apart the last fragment of the Last Prophecy with her own hands, unable to accept how it ends (see [[Susie]]) — the same pattern of bleeding right at a breaking point of refusal. Toby Fox has never confirmed that this means Deltarune's monsters can bleed as a rule, but the parallel between the two scenes — an "out of place" monster in each game, bleeding at its own moment of rupture — is one of the most repeated in community analysis.
>
> **A very widespread theory with an unusually strong body of evidence for a fan theory — but Toby Fox has never directly confirmed or denied it.**

> [!question]- Is Undertale the result of a Deltarune Weird Route?
> A much more speculative reading, but a recurring one in the fandom: that the world of Undertale itself — monsters sealed underground after a war with humans — isn't just any parallel universe, but the result of a Deltarune Weird Route being carried to its ultimate consequences at some point in the story, generating a completely different and much darker reality from those decisions. Under this reading, the "Angel's Heaven" that must be banished in the Prophecy (see [[Ángel|Angel]]) and Undertale's own human-monster war could be the same catastrophe seen from two different angles. **A very speculative theory with no direct textual support — it rests mostly on the thematic parallel between both conflicts, not on any concrete clue in the game.**

> [!question]- Is Deltarune a hidden prequel, not a parallel universe?
> Another minority reading: given that all of Hometown's residents "wear Undertale's face" but remember nothing of that life — no war, no Underground, no barrier — some fans read this not as two parallel lines but as the same world at an earlier point in time, before any of Undertale's events came to pass. This would fit with the fact that SOULs already exist as a concept in Deltarune without anyone yet using them in the "wartime" way they're used in Undertale. **A minority theory; Toby Fox has described the relationship between the two games as one of parallel universes, which doesn't fully fit with a strictly prequel reading.**

> [!question]- Why does the file remember what the characters shouldn't?
> Certain texts and symbols — Wingdings, references to "Entry Number 17" — appear in Deltarune without any character mentioning or justifying them within the fiction, as if they were part of the game engine's own "memory" rather than the story. It's one of the fandom's most-cited pieces of meta-narrative, with no official explanation.

## Trivia
- Toriel and Asgore remain divorced from Undertale, but here both are biological parents of Asriel and (by adoption, in Toriel's case, toward Kris) of Kris as well — a notable change in circumstances compared to the original game.
- The very name "Deltarune" is an anagram of "Undertale," the first explicit nod to the connection between the two titles.
- Undertale's own Gerson (in his shop in Waterfall) already mentioned the Prophecy and the Angel years before Deltarune existed — one of the oldest textual connections in the whole saga, present since the original game.
