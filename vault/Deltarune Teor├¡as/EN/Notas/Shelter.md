> [!info]+ Quick facts
> 🚪 Original name: *Shelter* (JP: シェルター, *Sherutā*). It's the southernmost point of the entire Hometown map.
> 🌲 The door hides an electronic panel with **3 code fields**, each next to a different symbol: a **pine tree**, a **police badge**, and the **Delta Rune** itself. To date, the first three digits of the pine tree code are known (**122**, found on Dess's guitar, Ch.4 — the fandom takes it for granted that the full number is **1225**) and the full police badge code (**0915199X**, hidden by Asgore in Flowery's flowerpot soil, Ch.5 — a reference to Undertale's release date, 9/15, and consistent with Asgore having been police chief before Undyne). The Delta Rune one still hasn't been found; in the game's files, the Delta Rune symbol itself is listed as one of the three codes, suggesting that field might not be filled in with digits but by "drawing" or selecting the rune itself.
> 🔒 According to Alphys, only a handful of people have direct access: she says she's only seen Undyne and Mayor Carol head there — the official wiki adds Kris and the Roaring Knight to that short list.
> 🐦 On the way there, you hear birdsong recycled from Undertale that fades out as Kris approaches the door, until it goes completely silent.
> 🔊 Under the door, `mus_smile` plays slowed to 15% — the same sound heard when using the phone inside a Dark World, and the same one that accompanies Gaster's Entry Number 17 in Undertale. This same sound, at the same speed, is also heard in the Holiday Mansion's basement — another environmental clue tying Carol to the place. See [[Gaster (W. D. Gaster)]].
> 🌑 Confirmed in Ch.3: the Shelter **contains its own Dark World** — inside it, Undyne has a confirmed Darkner form (combat armor and cyan skin, just like her Undertale counterpart). See [[Undyne]].
> ⛪ When asked about the Shelter in Ch.4, Father Alvin asks Kris to drop the subject and stay away from the area.

## Chapter-by-chapter history

> [!example]+ Chapter 1 — Silence at the door
> The Shelter is already visitable from the first chapter, though there's nothing to do there yet: as you approach, the birdsong from the path fades to total silence in front of the closed door.

> [!example]+ Chapter 2 — Rumors and a kick
> Snowy and Monster Kid, by the door, discuss rumors that Kris himself has been dropping about the place. Susie, hearing them, startles them by kicking the closed door — the first on-screen sign that the group already has real curiosity about the site.

## The Shelter's Dark World
Confirmed in Ch.3: beneath the closed door there's a Dark World of its own, of which so far only what's seen through Undyne's transformation is known — her police uniform turns into combat armor with gauntlets and spears, and her skin takes on a bright cyan tone, just like her Undertale counterpart.

## Theories

> [!question]+ What really happened at the Shelter?
> It's, together with the Lake, the biggest loose end in the entire series looking ahead to future chapters: it's unknown what specific incident happened here, exactly how it relates to Dess's disappearance, and what role the Roaring Knight will play, having brought both Undyne and, presumably, Asgore here. No specific theory has enough support yet — it's open ground.

> [!question]- The pattern of the two captures
> The Roaring Knight has brought its only two confirmed victims to date to the Shelter: Undyne (Ch.3) and, presumably, Asgore (Ch.5) — in both cases, after switching targets at the last moment (Toriel → Undyne; the rest of the party → Asgore). It's too repeated a pattern to be a coincidence, though the game hasn't yet explained what the Knight is trying to achieve by taking them specifically here.

> [!question]- 🎲 "Depths" and "Hell": the nod from the thank-you survey
> In the third round of the game development's own "Thank You" survey, participant responses related to "Shelter", "Depths", or "Hell" all received exactly the same hidden reply from the team — suggesting the three concepts are connected somehow in the development's own mind. "Depths" and "Hell" had already been connected before through Jevil's line [TODO: verify exact EN quote] "the Roar bubbles up from the depths" (see [[Cristal Oscuro|Shadow Crystal]]), and "Depth" also appears in the recurring internal file name IMAGE_DEPTH. This is a datamine/development-based data point, not confirmation within the game itself, so it counts as a speculative reading.
>
> ![[shelter_depths_hell.png]]
> *The real, community-compiled list of response variants that trigger the same hidden text: "Shelter", "The Depths", "Over the cauldron of hell", "Bunker"...*

> [!question]- 🎲 The number 1225 and the Holiday family
> The partial code found on Dess's guitar, "122", is interpreted almost universally as the start of "1225" — and that same number reappears repeatedly throughout the game in contexts tied to Noelle and the Cyber World, in addition to being used to open a secret room in the Ch.3 Dark World's plush machine and the path to the Christmas tree where Tenna was hiding Toriel. The family surname, "Holiday", and 1225's own Christmas meaning (December 25th) make the fandom read it as a deliberate numeric motif tied to this family — but the game never explains why that specific number opens the Shelter in particular.

> [!question]- 🎲 Is it related to Gaster?
> The musical coincidence (`mus_smile` at 15%, the same speed and the same file as in Undertale's Entry 17) is real and verifiable, and it's reinforced by the fact that the same sound also appears in the Holiday Mansion's basement — but at no point does the game confirm in text that Gaster has anything to do with the Shelter. For now, it's a reading based on sound and file clues, not dialogue.

> [!question]- 🎲 Is it Asriel investigating alongside Carol?
> There's a minority branch of the Asriel theory (see [[Roaring Knight]]) that imagines him secretly collaborating with Carol to search for Dess on his own — it would explain why Carol investigates the area so insistently. It rests on a single thread of community speculation, with no direct textual clue.

## Trivia
- It's one of the few places in the game locked in a completely physical way (a door with an electronic lock) instead of narratively.
- The fact that both Carol and Asgore have an almost identical investigation corkboard about this area is one of the fandom's most discussed environmental clues.
- The code "0915199X" is a direct nod to Undertale's release date (September 15, 2015); the final "X" is never revealed.
- The game's files literally tag the door as "shelter" in its internal object name (`obj_town_shelter`).
- The Delta Rune itself —a circle, two wings, and three triangles— isn't just the Prophecy's symbol: in the game's files, it's also listed as one of this same door's three codes.
