*Table note: "Mike" isn't a single character with his own talksprite — he's the name three impostors fight over. The real Mike never appears on screen, but as of Ch.5 the game itself confirms he did exist.*

> [!info]+ Quick facts
> 🎭 "Mike" is Tenna's personal assistant and business partner, and also a mutual friend of Spamton's — but he's never seen in person in any chapter so far.
> 🕵️ An optional Ch.4 fight reveals that his identity has been taken on by a trio of impostors: Small Mike (a green Pippins), Cat Mike (a Shadowguy), and Cowboy Hat Mike (a Zapper), who take turns playing the role so Tenna won't notice his absence.
> 🐾 Tenna clearly prefers Cat Mike/Pluey — who, not by coincidence, looks a lot like FRIEND.
> ☎️ Ch.5 finally confirms that the real Mike truly existed: he was the one who warned Tenna that Spamton shouldn't sign the contract or "something terrible" would happen, and he disappeared right after Tenna ignored him and everything went wrong anyway. Tenna even admits that, ever since, he felt Mike wasn't the same anymore or "felt fake."
> 🎮 After defeating the three impostors, their minigames unlock: Battat, Jongler, and Pluey.
> 🚪 If every possible Darkner is recruited, the door to Mike's room in Castle Town opens with the code 6453 — which spells "MIKE" on a phone keypad.

> [!tip]- Personality profile
> There's no single "Mike" personality — each impostor has his own. Small Mike is the most talkative and nervous of the trio (hence "Motormouth Mike"), gets defensive the moment the real Mike is mentioned, takes the role very seriously and scolds his companions for playing theirs badly, and keeps drinking a glass of something green even after finding out it's acid, just because it's green. Cat Mike is quieter, mostly just dances and listens, and is the one Tenna prefers. Cowboy Hat Mike doesn't even bother memorizing his lines and tends to mix up his costume whenever someone unexpected shows up. Almost nothing else is known about the real Mike beyond Tenna always remembering him as a [TODO: verify exact EN quote] "prankster."

> [!example]+ Chapter 5 — Tenna's confession and the fandom's own parody
> Tenna finally gives more context about the real Mike: he confirms Mike was his close friend and business partner, and also a mutual friend of Spamton's, and that he warned him that Spamton couldn't sign the contract being demanded of him or "something terrible" [TODO: verify exact EN quote] would happen — whether because Mike sensed it, or because he knew something more. Tenna, in a rare moment of honesty, admits he knew forcing Spamton to sign was a bad idea, and instead blames Spamton's anonymous benefactor for "abandoning him" rather than owning up to his own responsibility.
>
> The trio of impostors also expands their role as a direct parody of the fan community itself — each one corresponds to a real, distinct theory about who Mike is: Small Mike/Battat parodies the theory that "Mike is the TV itself" (born after the end of Ch.2, before Tenna's name was revealed in the Spamton Sweepstakes); Cat Mike/Pluey represents the theory that Mike is FRIEND in disguise; and Cowboy Hat Mike/Jongler mocks the theory that Mike was Spamton's anonymous benefactor. Battat further claims that the "Freedom" motif from the soundtrack actually belongs to them (calling it the "BATTAT motif" or "AIR WAVES motif" depending on the console), and gets into an argument with the Trash Trio over the same motif. They also show up at the end of the fight against that Trio, later joining another confrontation alongside Lanino and Elnina that ends in a draw when Rouxls Kaard shows up.

## Theories

> [!question]+ Is the real Mike FRIEND?
> The most popular theory about Mike: FRIEND is referenced several times during the battle against the impostors (and even appears in person as an easter egg in the Battat minigame), and the real Mike's behavior — warning about the contract, disappearing when ignored — would fit with not wanting to lose a good "puppet" (Spamton). If Spamton's anonymous benefactor is FRIEND or Gaster himself, it would make sense for FRIEND not to want to lose that influence. Ch.5 confirms Mike existed and warned Tenna, which reinforces the theory's foundation — but it never confirms that Mike and FRIEND are the same entity; that part still has no direct textual support.

> [!question]- Each impostor parodies a different fandom theory
> Battat (Small Mike) grew out of the early theory that "Mike is the TV itself" — his smile recalls Tenna's at the end of Ch.2, before his name was revealed in the Spamton Sweepstakes; Pluey (Cat Mike) represents the theory that Mike is FRIEND in disguise; and Jongler (Cowboy Hat Mike) mocks the theory that Mike was Spamton's anonymous benefactor. The three impostors don't just fill Mike's narrative void — they literally embody the three most popular theories the fandom held about him before Ch.5.

## Trivia
- Small Mike is the only Pippins in the whole game that's green.
- In the Japanese version, Small Mike speaks in the Kansai dialect like the rest of the TV World's Pippins, but his accent slips out when he tries to sound more "standard" while pretending to be Mike.
- The code for Mike's room door, 6453, spells "MIKE" on a phone keypad.
- The giant photorealistic microphone hidden in his room's hallway is, according to the fandom itself, a possible pun between "real mic" and "real Mike."
- The three impostors appear as playable characters in the non-canon Spamton Sweepstakes spin-off: the trio as the "Mike Gang" racer, and Small Mike/Battat as a solo survivor.
