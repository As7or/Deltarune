## The Fun Value and the True Lab
Almost everything "secret" tied to Gaster — the three Followers, the Sound Room, the Fake Hallway with Mystery Man — depends on the same internal mechanism: the **Fun Value**, a random number between 1 and 100 that the game assigns when the save file is started or reset. Each rare event requires an exact value (or range) to appear, and most of them — on triggering, or even just from entering the right room without triggering — reset that value to 0, making it impossible to repeat within the same save without hand-editing the save file. It is, in practice, an entire layer of optional content based on pure luck, and almost all of its events point back to Gaster in one way or another.

![[gaster_fun_values.png]]
*A fan-made table of the different Fun Values and the events each one unlocks — almost all of them tied to Gaster.*

The **True Lab**, accessible after completing enough of Alphys's entries about Determination, is the setting for most of these clues: that's where Room 264 (`room_gaster`) with Entry 17 is located, and where the three Followers are found, near the Hotland elevators, each with its own exact Fun Value (62, 61, and 63) and an appearance chance that never reaches 100%.

With the sole exception of the third, each Follower is a grey, nameless version of an NPC that normally exists elsewhere in the game — someone who, coincidentally, also had their own small "accident" caused by fear, a thematic parallel with Gaster himself that the fandom doesn't consider a coincidence.

## Clam Girl and Susie — the clue nobody expected
In the Switch version of Undertale (released a month before Deltarune's Ch.1), Clam Girl — an optional Waterfall NPC — mentions her neighbor's daughter, "Suzy," and encourages the player to become her friend, though she never actually appears on screen. With an exact Fun Value of 81, her dialogue changes in the epilogue: she says the moment of meeting Suzy [TODO: verify exact EN quote] "is coming soon," while her sprite turns grey — and at the end of the conversation she vanishes with the same sound used by Mystery Man and Gaster's second Follower. Her new sprite is internally named `spr_clam_goner`.

![[gaster_gonerclampgirl.webp]]
*Clam Girl transforming into her grey version, "Clam Goner," right before disappearing.*

As further support, talking to her unlocks a drawing in Sans's workshop with the inscription "don't forget" — the title of Ch.1's credits song and its main leitmotif. The fandom's conclusion is that "Suzy" is none other than Susie herself, and that Gaster — through this nearly invisible NPC — had already been hinting at her existence before Deltarune even existed.

![[gaster_and_deltarune_clampgirl_goner_speech.webp]]
*Clam Girl's full dialogue about "Suzy," the most cited clue for this connection.*

## The Voice, Toby Fox's tweets, and "the experiment"
The day before Ch.1's release, Undertale's official Twitter accounts (English and Japanese) emptied out their name and avatar and started tweeting in all caps, in the same spaced-out, "very, very interesting" tone as Entry 17 — speaking in first person as if they were the very Voice that opens Deltarune, saying they had been looking for the player and had something to show them. Toby Fox, around that time, also referred to Deltarune as an "experiment."

## Theories

> [!question]+ Who are "the two of you" in Entry 17's final question?
> That question is never answered within the game itself. The most widespread reading, with the most supporting clues, is that Gaster is addressing **Sans and Papyrus**: Sans has access to a "Gaster Blaster" as his own attack, there are blueprints and a covered machine in his house in Snowdin, and Sans's wrong-number song is looking for someone whose name starts with "G." Another, more minority reading argues that Gaster, Sans, and Papyrus are actually outsiders from another world — leaning on the fact that the Waterfall shopkeeper mentions the brothers "just showed up one day" in town, with no further explanation — which would fit with Gaster's own nature of "being outside of time and space."

> [!question]+ Is the intro Voice Gaster himself?
> This is, by far, the strongest of all the theories related to him. In favor: the tone and format of the tweets match, point by point, the Voice's within the game; both quote Entry 17 almost verbatim ("very, very interesting"); the "Vessel" created in the intro is internally labeled "Goner," the same term used for the rest of the grey characters tied to Gaster; and the Voice's font value is 666. Against it: it's never directly confirmed within the game itself, not even indirectly, by full name. **An extremely well-supported fan theory; not 100% confirmed by the script.**

> [!question]- What is his true appearance?
> None of his "visible" forms is confirmed as his real appearance: Mystery Man is a deformed, cracked sprite with no collision, more a broken silhouette than a finished design; REDACTED, his equivalent in Deltarune, is directly censored on screen; and neither one is ever shown in a normal playthrough without tricks. The most widespread reading is that he'd be a skeleton, to match Sans and Papyrus if they're his sons, but that's an inference from kinship, not a design confirmed in any game file. **Unconfirmed; his true appearance, if he has one, remains a deliberate mystery on the part of the game itself.**

> [!question]- Is he the Forgotten Man?
> This is the most popular reading of the [[Forgotten Man]]'s identity: he moves freely between the Light World and the Dark, speaks in timeless terms about the world's origin, fits the description of someone "shattered across space and time," and the room where he appears in Ch.1 shares its structure with Mystery Man's room in Undertale. Against it: his way of speaking in Ch.3 is noticeably different from the Voice's, which makes part of the fandom doubt they share an identity. See [[Forgotten Man]] for the full discussion, including the other two alternative readings of who he is. **A fan theory, the most popular one about the Forgotten Man's identity; unconfirmed.**

> [!question]- Is Deltarune Gaster's "experiment," and is Undertale the result of a "weird route"?
> Putting several pieces together — that Toby Fox called Deltarune an "experiment" before its release, that the Voice/Gaster talks about "showing something" to the player, and that Deltarune's entire premise revolves around an "Angel" controlling Kris from outside — part of the fandom speculates with a meta reading: that Deltarune would be, within its own fiction, an experiment by Gaster himself to observe how "the player" behaves under different conditions, and that Undertale could be read retroactively as a prior simulation, or even as the result of one of his "weird routes." It's one of the most speculative theories that exist about him — a nice meta-narrative reading, but with no direct textual support within either game. **A highly speculative fan theory; unconfirmed.**

## References and clues by chapter (Deltarune)
This section brings together, chapter by chapter, everything within Deltarune itself that has been connected to Gaster — to keep it clearly separate from the clues that come from Undertale (Followers, Mystery Man, Entry 17, Clam Girl), gathered above.

> [!example]+ Chapter 1
> - The chapter's installer version is **0.6.6.6** — the first appearance of the 666 tied to him within Deltarune.
> - Everyman first appears hidden in Jevil's carousel attack (1-in-50 odds) — he shares an internal naming pattern with Gaster: of the few names that end in "-man" in Undertale's files, his is *mysteryman* and Everyman's is *strangeman*, both tied to the True Lab. See [[Everyman]].

> [!example]+ Chapter 2
> - The Ficus Licker — the "normal" base of Undertale's Follower 1 — reappears, now in color, sitting in a booth at the QC café.
> - Everyman appears again: in Poppup's pop-up-window attack, and in Queen's "Social Media" attack, reusing the same sprite that would later be used in the Spamton Sweepstakes.

> [!example]+ Chapter 3 (compiled from an analysis video, cross-checked against the wiki where possible)
> - **The missing G**: when choosing the player's initials in Tenna's show, the letter G is the only one excluded from the selection for the third slot — coincidentally the exact position it would occupy in "WDG" (W. D. Gaster).
> - **Absence of G/A sounds**: in Ch.1 and 2 there were internal sound effects simply named "W" and "D"; in Ch.3 and 4 no sound file named "G" or "A" has been found — whether this absence is intentional (saving the "G" for elsewhere, like the initials selection) remains unconfirmed.
> - **The grey with a value of 666**: when Tenna thinks the group doesn't want to take part in the contest, the show's sign turns grey — a grey whose decimal color value is exactly 666.
> - **"crowd/love" sound**: the sound effect for FRIEND's scare in the cutscene where Ralsei explains the Dark Worlds is internally named "crowd/love" and plays up to four times in that same scene, at different pitches and speeds — even though the "crowd" laughter heard afterward in other scenes uses a different file name.
> - **Northern Light at 66% and Glacier at 1332%**: inside the Ice Palace (Sword Route), the music "Northern Light" plays slowed to 66%, and later, inside the Shelter itself in its 8-bit recreation, "Glacier" (the Ice Palace's own music) plays slowed to 1332% — exactly double 666.
> - **The "smile" machine**: [TODO: verify exact EN quote] at a backstage vending machine you can buy an item called "smile," and the game responds "at your humble service" — this may allude to the `smile` sound from Entry 17 itself, and the "humble service" phrase recalls [TODO: verify exact EN quote] a cut line from a demon in Undertale's code ("I, your humble servant, will follow you to the end").
> - **Real merchandise with Gaster's internal naming**: the three official Susie, Ralsei, and Kris Mini figures use the same internal file-naming format as product names that the weapons tied to the Voice/Gaster use in the game's code: "Hero/Scarf," "Hero/Sword," and "Hero/Axe."
> - **Ramb and Mystery Man**: according to leaked design lists, Ramb shares a moderately similar face to Mystery Man's, along with Jevil and Rouxls Kaard.
> - Mancountry's (Forgotten Island's) hidden areas use a completely monochrome floor and aesthetic, the same "colorless" palette tied to him in Undertale.
> - The "smile noise" (`mus_smile`), slowed to 15%, plays next to the Shelter door — confirmed as a Dark World this same chapter. See [[Shelter]].
>
> ![[gaster_mancountry_binoculars_grey_floor.webp]]
> *The grey floor of one of Mancountry's hidden areas, Ch.3.*
>
> *Rigor note: most of this list consists of numerical and file-naming coincidences, not dialogue or direct confirmation from the game — they fit the pattern already established in this note (666, grey, "forgetting"), but each isolated data point is weak on its own. They're included because they fit an already extensively documented pattern, not as new independent evidence.*

> [!example]+ Chapter 4
> - There's an unused internal variable tied to Everyman specific to this chapter, with no known function — see [[Everyman]] for details.
> - FRIEND appears for the first time, barely visible among the water ripples of the Second Sanctuary's flooded hallway. Its internal names, `DEVICE_FRIEND` and `IMAGE_FRIEND`, share the same `DEVICE_` prefix and the same all-caps convention as the resources tied to the Voice/Gaster.

> [!example]+ Chapter 5
> - FRIEND reappears, lurking in the Garden — the encounter that later unlocks Pink's secret dialogue about hunting cats.
> - Although not a Gaster clue in itself, Pink's scene in this chapter is the first textual confirmation of the Kris's-hand theory — see [[Kris]].

## Trivia
- His full name, "W. D. Gaster," is almost certainly a play on words with "Wingdings," the font he speaks in.
- The audio file for his theme in Undertale is internally named `mus_st_him`; Deltarune's intro theme is named `ANOTHER HIM` — the same keyword, "him," in both.
- 🚫 If you type "GASTER" as the vessel's or creator's name in the intro, the game resets to the start of the chapter — just like happens in Undertale when you name the fallen human "Gaster."
- 🔗 He shares file-naming patterns with other minor mysteries: the same "-man" prefix as Everyman, the same total absence on screen as Papyrus, and the DEVICE_ prefix also used by FRIEND.
- There was an official Gaster collectible card that was pulled and replaced with Gerson Boom once the collection became an official license — Toby Fox himself hinted that this change means the original card isn't canon.
