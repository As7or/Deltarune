*The SOUL, the element that directly controls the Player — not Kris.*

*Table note: the Player has no sprite of their own, but the game represents them mechanically through the SOUL, the red heart that moves independently of Kris himself.*

> [!info]+ Quick facts
> 🕹️ Is the real person controlling Kris through the SOUL — moves the character, navigates menus, chooses dialogue, and decides battle actions.
> 👻 The game itself mechanically confirms that the Player and Kris are separate entities: several times throughout the series, Kris tears the SOUL from their chest and acts on their own, leaving the Player with no control at all.
> 🎙️ In the Chapter 1 intro, a bodiless voice directly asks "are you connected?" [TODO: verify exact EN quote] before guiding the Player to design a "VESSEL" and name it — and then asks for the real name of whoever is playing.
> 🗑️ Right after, a second entity interrupts the creation of that Vessel, declares it will be "discarded" [TODO: verify exact EN quote] ("no one can choose who they are in this world" [TODO: verify exact EN quote]) and assigns the Player's connection to Kris instead — without making clear who this second voice is.
> ❄️ The Chapter 2 Snowgrave Route is the most-cited example of this tension: Noelle goes so far as to question out loud whether it's really Kris doing "this," hinting that she senses the difference between Kris and whoever is controlling them.

> [!tip]- Personality profile
> They have no personality of their own within the fiction — they are, literally, whoever is on the other side of the screen. The game treats them at times as an almost invasive presence (the initial voice, the very name it asks for), and at other times as an entity Kris tolerates, ignores, or openly rejects depending on the moment.

## Where they appear

> [!example]+ Chapter 1 — Creating the Vessel (Goner Maker)
> Before Kris exists, with the game window literally titled "CONTACT," a bodiless voice asks the Player if they're "connected" [TODO: verify exact EN quote]. A red SOUL appears, and the voice guides the construction of a body — a "VESSEL" — choosing a head (8 options), body (6), and legs (5): every selectable piece appears in grayscale. It also asks what "gift" it should have (MIND by default) and two yes/no questions about accepting "the possibility of pain and convulsions" [TODO: verify exact EN quote] — answering no to either changes nothing: the voice replies "UNDERSTOOD" [TODO: verify exact EN quote] and continues regardless, giving no real chance to back out.
>
> Finally, it asks to name the Vessel (up to 9 characters, e.g. "DELTARUNE") and, afterward, the real name of whoever is playing (up to 12, capitals only) — breaking the fourth wall more directly than anywhere else in the game. Typing "GASTER" as either name resets the intro entirely, a direct nod to how, in Undertale, naming the fallen human that same way sent the player back to the prologue.
>
> Just as the voice is about to say the chosen name out loud, the scene cuts abruptly to Kris's room, with Toriel shouting their name instead — as if the narration itself interrupted the voice before it could finish the sentence. Shortly after, the text style changes (lowercase, no glow, no ambient sound) to announce that the Vessel will be "discarded," because "no one can choose who they are in this world" [TODO: verify exact EN quote] — and the Player's connection is reassigned to Kris. That first Vessel's data doesn't fully disappear: it stays saved within the save file itself, never used again.
>
> At the end of the chapter, after sealing the first Dark Fountain, Kris tears his own SOUL from his chest and throws it into a cage — the Player can move the SOUL inside the cage, but not Kris, making clear that it's the SOUL, not Kris, that they actually control.

> [!example]+ Chapter 2 — Snowgrave and the most-cited separation
> If the Snowgrave Route is completed, Noelle directly questions whether it's really Kris doing what's happening — one of the few times a character verbalizes out loud the suspicion that someone else is behind Kris's decisions. At the end of the chapter, Kris tears out the SOUL again and acts alone, this time opening a new Dark Fountain with their own knife.

> [!example]+ Chapter 4 — the Weird Route and the "punished" SOUL
> Only accessible if the Chapter 2 Weird Route was already completed. Kris uses the SOUL to travel alone, through the air vents, to Noelle's room at her house — while Kris's body stays behind elsewhere in the house, completely separate. Noelle says Kris came to see her at night to try to fix what happened on the Chapter 2 Weird Route, and that they pulled out the thorn stuck in her finger (the Light World counterpart of the ThornRing) — she says she's glad to hear their "serious, muttering" [TODO: verify exact EN quote] voice again, and asks if they can say something more: the Player chooses between "Let it happen" or "Please don't" [TODO: verify exact EN quote]. If "Let it happen" is chosen, the SOUL can re-enter the room and return to Kris's body.
>
> Before that, Noelle asks out loud who they're worried might hear what she's saying — while Kris tries unsuccessfully to cover her mouth. The Player can choose the option "Me" [TODO: verify exact EN quote], revealing itself once again as a separate entity directly in front of Noelle.
>
> If the route isn't aborted, Kris drives the ThornRing back into Noelle's hand and, once in the house's bathroom, **tears out the SOUL, throws it in the trash can, and kicks it repeatedly with genuine rage** — until Asgore interrupts to announce that Carol wants everyone to leave. Taking the first step after this, Kris falls to the ground before getting back up; their HP drops to 10 as a direct result of the beating they (or the SOUL) just took.
>
> ![[Kris_screenshot_kicking_SOUL.png]]
> *Kris, now without the SOUL, kicking it inside the bathroom trash can — Ch.4, Weird Route.*
>
> On the way back to the church, Susie asks Kris what happened with Noelle: a choice box appears with **four completely blank options**, while the rain disappears, the sound cuts out, and the screen turns pink for the entire choice — one of the most unsettling and least explained moments in the whole Weird Route.

> [!example]+ Chapter 5 — New separations
> The pattern repeats: Kris separates from the Player again while creating the Flower Kingdom's Fountain without Susie seeing, and at several other points during the chapter. In the church, Tasque Manager addresses Kris by name directly — one of the few times a cast member seems to recognize them as an individual, not just the Player's "Vessel."

## Catalog — every time Kris kicks us out of their body
A quick, chapter-by-chapter rundown of every confirmed moment where Kris tears out the SOUL and acts without the Player:
- **Ch.1**: at the end, after sealing the first Fountain, tears out the SOUL and throws it into a cage — wields a knife, smirks maliciously, and their eye flashes red (with a sprite recycled from Asgore in Undertale).
- **Ch.2**: returning home after the Cyber World, instead of washing their hands they tear out the SOUL and throw it in the sink. If the Weird Route was completed, they tear it out again at the end of the chapter to open a new Fountain with their own knife.
- **Ch.4 (normal route)**: hides it in a gift box in the guest room at Noelle's house before speaking with her alone; on the Weird Route, they also use it to travel alone through the air vents (see above) and end up kicking it inside a trash can.
- **Ch.5**: tears it out again to create the Flower Kingdom's Fountain without Susie noticing, and at other scattered points in the chapter.

## The Voice — who's speaking at the start?

> [!info]+ Quick profile
> 🎙️ A still-unseen character who speaks directly to the Player during the Vessel's creation in the Chapter 1 intro — one of the few characters in the whole game to actively acknowledge the Player's existence.
> 🔠 Always speaks in all caps, at a slow, deliberate pace, in a formal, sparse tone — the same style used by the achievements/trophies on the PS4 and PS5 versions.
> 🕹️ Speaks again if there's a Game Over in Chapter 1: acknowledges that "it looks like you've reached an end" [TODO: verify exact EN quote] and asks if you want to try again, assuring that "the future is in your hands" [TODO: verify exact EN quote].
> 🗂️ The Vessel creation sequence is internally called **GONERMAKER**, and the Vessel itself is labeled in the game's files as **"GONER."**

> [!question]+ Is the Voice W. D. Gaster?
> One of the most solid, best archive-supported theories in the whole game — nicknamed by the community "the Gaster List":
> - The sequence's own internal name, **GONERMAKER**, and the Vessel labeled **"GONER,"** connect directly to Undertale's "Goners" (Goner Kid, Goner Clam Girl) — gray NPCs already associated with Gaster, who vanish with the same sound effect as him and his Followers.
> - The Vessel is generated in grayscale, just like those same NPCs right before they disappear.
> - The intro theme, "ANOTHER HIM," shares a leitmotif with the "Gaster Theme" (internally labeled `mus_st_him` in Undertale) — both use the same Chrono Trigger soundfont piano.
> - Typing "GASTER" as the Vessel's or Player's own name resets the intro — the same trick as calling the fallen human "Gaster" in Undertale.
> - Before Deltarune was even announced, the official Undertale Twitter account went blank and tweeted, in the same style as the Voice, that it had something to show that it found "VERY, VERY INTERESTING" [TODO: verify exact EN quote] — an almost literal quote from Gaster's Entry Number Seventeen in Undertale.
> - According to the theory known as "Gaster's List," every name that gets a special response from the Voice when typed into the sequence belongs, almost without exception, to a real Lightner from Hometown — as if whoever is asking already knew, or was cataloguing, the real people of that world.
>
> **A widely held theory with substantial archive support, but never confirmed by the game itself or by Toby Fox.**
>

>
> ![[gaster_gonerclampgirl.webp]]
> *Clam Girl turning into her gray version, "Clam Goner," right before disappearing — the same visual pattern.*

> [!question]- Are the Voice and the "second voice" that discards the Vessel the same entity?
> It's not entirely clear. All the "Gaster's List" evidence rests on the part of the sequence where the Vessel is built and named — but the final segment, the one declaring the Vessel will be "discarded" because "no one can choose who they are in this world," changes text style (lowercase, no glow, no ambience) right before assigning the connection to Kris. Part of the community reads this as a second, distinct entity interrupting the first; another part reads it as the same Voice shifting register for a more solemn announcement. **No official confirmation either way.**

> [!question]- Does Gaster create Vessels as part of an experiment?
> A more speculative extension of the theory above: if the Voice is Gaster, it's possible that the very process of creating a Vessel isn't just a metanarrative nod to the player, but a real experiment within the fiction — Gaster generating empty, monochrome bodies (like the Goners themselves) to house something, with the Player as just one more of those test subjects, and Kris as the process's final, already "stabilized" result. This would connect with the theory, already covered in the [[Clam Guy]] note, that Susie could be linked to Suzy — the Clam Girl who turns into her gray "Clam Goner" version right before disappearing — as part of the same pattern of Gaster possessing or using other people's bodies as vessels. **Speculative fan theory, built on top of another unconfirmed theory — two layers of "unconfirmed" stacked on each other.**

## Theories

> [!question]+ Is the Player, in fact, the Angel of the Prophecy?
> The most widespread reading: the Angel wouldn't be a cast character, but a narrative personification of whoever is in charge from outside the fiction — that is, the person playing. It would fit the saga's own metanarrative design (the initial Voice, the discarded Vessel, the real name asked of the player) and with the Prophecy describing the Angel as someone with "a wish that they will finally get once banished" [TODO: verify exact EN quote] — possibly the wish to know how the story ends. Under this reading, "banishing" the Angel would mean the Player losing control over Kris at the end of the series. **A strong, widely held theory, though the game never confirms it in these exact words — see also the [[Ángel|Angel]] note.**

> [!question]- Is the Player, in fact, Chara?
> Since Undertale's Genocide ending is canon within that same continuity, and Chara bids farewell to that playthrough saying "there's nothing left for us here, let's erase this pointless world and move on to the next" [TODO: verify exact EN quote], part of the fandom theorizes that Deltarune could literally be "the next world" — with Kris as Chara's/the Player's new Vessel. **A widespread fan theory that depends on assuming which Undertale ending was carried over into the save file — with no direct confirmation within Deltarune.**

> [!question]- Who is the second voice that discards the first Vessel?
> At the start of Chapter 1, a second entity — distinct from the voice guiding the creation of the first Vessel — interrupts the process, declares that Vessel will be "discarded," and assigns the Player's connection to Kris. Its identity is never clarified: the most-cited theories point to Gaster, to the "Voice" that already appeared in Undertale, or to an entirely new entity unrelated to known characters. In the Japanese version, this second voice speaks in kanji and hiragana, a detail various analyses have used to try to identify it, without a conclusive result. **No official confirmation.**

## Trivia
- The real name asked of the Player during the first Vessel's creation is saved within the save file, even though that Vessel is never used again as such.
- Tasque Manager, in the Chapter 5 church, is one of the few times a cast member addresses Kris by name directly — a small contrast with the rest of the game, which treats Kris almost always as the Player's generic "Vessel."
